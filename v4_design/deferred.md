# Deferred

Work that has been looked at and set aside for now: worth doing, but not yet. Each item says what it would take and why it waits. Items that are done move to `implementation.md`.

## Performance

- **The rest of the deliveries in a whole run.** Deliveries on a schedule of a product people hold, or a transfer, to people chosen by states and stratifications, are taken by the kernels (`ss.Delivery.tabulated`). Still stepped from Python, at 120 to 300 µs a step against about 10 µs: tests and their follow-ups (a care cascade, as tbsim's `care()`), treatments that cure (moving people along a transition, credited to its flow), capacity, `when=`, deliveries in a model of several diseases, modifiers with `when=` or a function as their amount, and transfers whose rate varies. A screen whose follow-ups are all compiled could be one set of moves from the compartments at the time of the screen (each person's path through the cascade is a product of static shares): shifts between groups as now, and moves along a transition to its destination's entry compartments (with branches, maps between dimensions, and the flow credited), drawn as a multinomial under the stochastic solvers; a `when=` that is a function of the sim cannot be compiled. Not done yet (2026-10-16), pending Cliff's decision: the cascade is the larger piece, and would let tbsim's care cascade be fitted by NUTS.
- **Dropping the empty groups of a ragged stratification.** With `where=` on `stratify()`, a compartmental run keeps the full grid of groups: the people outside `where=` are counted at the first level, and the groups at the other levels stay empty but are still worked out (four age groups of women and one of men is eight groups, not five). Keeping only the groups that can hold people would need the group index to go through a lookup wherever it is now worked out by arithmetic (`group_mults`), in the solvers, kernels, movers, and fitting. Worth it only if a real model has many empty groups.
- **The rest of an agent-based step.** After this round, what is left in a large agent-based step is spread out: the infection pass, exposure by place, and finding the members of each state (one `flatnonzero` a state), each 1 to 3 ms a step for 668,000 agents. Finding the members of every state in one pass gains little, since each state is its own array.

- **The particle filter on outcomes** (review W11). With an outcome such as `cases` as the data, `engine.collect(..., expected=True)` works out the whole run again at every data point, about 2.5 times slower than with a flow. It would take keeping the outcome's pending amounts between data points. It waits for the particles-in-the-kernel work (N27, phase 8), which changes the same code.
- **Threads in parallel workers.** NumPy's BLAS and Numba each start a pool of threads in every process. For one process this costs nothing (measured on the transition tests: the same CPU time, 1.5 to 1.7 times faster in wall time than with one thread), but many processes at once (pytest-xdist, `ss.parallel()`) oversubscribe the cores. It would take setting one thread in each worker (as `SCIRIS_NUM_THREADS=1` does for BLAS). It waits for a measurement of `ss.parallel()` with and without it.

- **Compiling the kernels by solver.** Each solver could compile only its own path through `ss.kernels.step()`, which would cut a first compiled run from about 45 s to 18 s (ODE) or 27 s (binomial) ([oct21/numba_vs_jax.md](oct21/numba_vs_jax.md)). Numba makes it awkward: a literal `solver` didn't drop the other paths and made calls 20× slower, and passing or closing over a step function defeats Numba's cache (its type, or its pickle, differs in every process). What works is a closure over the solver as a number (Numba drops the other branches and caches each), with each caller reaching the solver's `step()` through an `if` on that number, or a module-level tuple of functions (an experimental Numba feature that can't inline). Set aside on 2026-10-21 (S5.11 of [oct21/plan.md](oct21/plan.md)), since the kernels now run as plain Python until compiling pays, so the compile is opt-in and happens once; worth it if the one-time compile (about 100 s for everything) becomes a problem, e.g. in CI.

## Accuracy

- **Continuous times of entering in the agent engine.** A declared disease run as agents and one written as code now round a time between steps the same way, at random in proportion by each agent's phase (`ss.People.phase()`: step floor(t + phase)). But the engine starts each stay at a whole step (the step of the move), and rounds each dwell from there, while code that chains its times (HIV's `ti_falling = ti_latent + dur_latent`) rounds the sum. So the same agent's successive stays are rounded the same way (with a fixed 1.5 steps, always 1 or always 2), and a chain of stays is rounded piece by piece rather than once. The exact version keeps each move's continuous time (the drawn exit time) as the next state's time of entering, schedules from it, and takes the exit at floor(t + phase): then both are one continuous-time process observed at the steps. It would take the scheduled exits as floats (`ti_exit_X` compared with the phase wherever it is now compared with `ti`, including the rescaling of a dwell whose rate changes and `backdate()`), and `schedule()` given the exit time on each scheduled move; it is not simpler or faster than the rounding by phase, and the difference is in how each agent's rounding errors are correlated, not in any mean, so it waits.
- **Thinning for time-varying rates under Gillespie.** Gillespie and tau-leaping hold a value that varies smoothly in time for a quarter of a step at a time (with a note), rather than thinning as the spec asks for. Thinning needs a bound on every rate over the step, which a rate written as any expression of the parameters does not give; a bound could be worked out by interval arithmetic over the kernels' operations, if exactness is needed.

## Features

- **Triangles as a target statistic.** `ss.PartnerNet` and `ss.MFNet` target the number of partners people have at a time and mixing by a property (`match=`). Triangles (two of a person's partners who are partners of each other), which an ERGM can also target, need the network fitted as a whole, not partner by partner.
- **WorldMove (synthetic movement within cities).** A collaborator suggested it ([Scientific Data, 2026](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41597-026-06555-2); https://github.com/tsinghua-fib-lab/WorldMove): one day of synthetic trajectories for each of about 1,600 cities in 179 countries, on a 1 km grid in half-hour slots, from a diffusion model, with commuting origin-destination matrices between the cells of each city. It is within cities, while `ss.geo` and `ss.gravity` are between administrative units, and a v4 run at a step of a day has no use for half-hourly paths. Its commuting matrices could give `ss.Mobility(matrix=...)` for a model of one city on a grid. It waits for a vignette that needs one; the repository has no license, and the paper is CC BY-NC-ND, so `starsim_data` could not redistribute it as things stand.
- **Gaps found while writing the importers and the import guides.** Each was worked around or listed as not translated. Fixed since (2026-10-18): `ss.PartnerNet(acts=)`, deaths from a life table (`ss.death(lifetable=)`), seeding a number of people on a schedule (a delivery with `move=` and `capacity=`), `ss.Mobility(scale='mean', cap=)`, the binomial solver's substeps for one Erlang stage (it now takes whole steps), and a one-off move between states (`ss.vaccinate(move='S -> V')`). Still open:
    - There is no declared condition on a network (a person's degree, or being the partner of someone infected).
    - A transition's current flow cannot be used in a rate (MEmilio's test-and-trace capacity): `I >> R @ (0.1 + 0.5*infection.incidence(over=ss.days(7)))` raises a `TypeError` ("condition has no single truth value").
    - `ss.PartnerNet` takes edges, concurrency, a cap on degree, matching on one attribute, and a number of partners by level (`n_contacts=ss.by(...)`, EpiModel's `nodefactor`); other ERGM terms (triangles, `nodematch(diff=TRUE)`) have no equivalent (EpiModel).
    - EpiModel lets people recover in the step they are infected, and counts after that, so its prevalence is 3 to 6% lower for the same epidemic; Starsim has no option for that order within a step.
    - An initial number below 1 is read as a fraction of the population, which a model with a fractional count (camdl's test models) cannot say otherwise.
    - In a compartmental run, a value that varies in time is taken at the start, middle, and end of each step, not at each substep, so a change within one step (MEmilio's one-day smoothing of a damping) is approximated, and `substeps=` does not help; a smaller `dt` does.
    - A count over the groups weighted by a parameter that differs between them (MEmilio's test-and-trace load, the sum over age groups of `(1 - mu)/T × INS`) cannot be written; `.n` is an unweighted total, so the importer uses the mean of the parameter.
    - A parameter of the infectious person's group (MEmilio's `RelativeTransmissionNoSymptoms` and `RiskOfInfectionFromSymptomatic` by age) can be given only as `rel_trans` for all infectious states together; a different one for each infectious state has no declared form.
- **`describe(verbose=True)`** (review P7, Decision docs 13, which recommends building it). It would show the per-step conversion of every converted quantity (`beta = 0.3 per day -> p = 1 - exp(-0.3 × 1) = 0.2592 per step`), the generated Erlang stages, and each schedule group's rows of the loop table, with a module moved by `sim.loop.insert()` at its new place (today the schedule ignores insertions). It would take a `verbose` argument on `Sim.describe()` and `Disease.describe()`, using the engine's dwell means and stage counts (`Engine.dwells`, `dwell_mean()`) and the loop's plan rather than the module list: about an hour with a test. It is simply not built yet; the spec no longer promises it.
- **The size of a stratified model, and its equations by group.** `describe()` lists each dimension's levels but not the number of compartments (groups times states), and `equations()` of a stratified disease prints the unstratified equations. The count is cheap (sum `sim.n_groups` times the states each dimension applies to, with a note above about 10,000 compartments, as the dropped `LargeStateSpace` warning was); an indexed form (`dS[a]/dt = -beta * S[a] * sum_b(C[a,b] * I[b] / N[b])`) needs the engine's groups and mixing written symbolically. Waits for a user who needs either: the sparse movers and factored mixing made large grids cheap.
- **The flow of each source of one transition.** A transition with several sources (`(S | I | R) >> D`) records only its total flow (deaths by the state they died from). The destination half is done (`res.cin.X`, 2026-10-19, `oct17/designs/destination_results.md`); a source would get a name of its own by the same mechanism, not a `source` axis, once sources can be named as targets. Waits for a user who needs it.
- **An outcome on the sim, across modules** (`ss.Sim([flu, rsv], ili=ss.outcome(0.3 * (flu.infection + rsv.infection)))`). The spec promised it; today `ili=` is an unknown parameter of the sim. It would take sim-level results built from several engines' flows by group, under every solver and in the kernels' whole runs and JAX, with data matching and likelihoods for a sim-level result. Waits because no vignette needs it: arithmetic on results after the run gives the series.
- **Fitting a product of parameters as one quantity** (`sim.fit(pars=[beta * contacts])`, which raises `ss.NothingToFit`). It would take `pars=` accepting an expression of `ss.par`s, with bounds from the factors, moving the first factor and holding the rest, and reporting the product. Waits because the `ss.Unidentifiable` note already says to fit one and fix the other.
- **A compartmental approximation of networks of lasting partnerships** (review X23). `ss.MFNet` and `ss.PartnerNet` under `ode`, `binomial`, or `gillespie` raise `ss.CapabilityError` ("no compartmental approximation yet"), although partner-change rates are the standard approximation for sexually transmitted infections. It would take partner-change rates between sex and risk groups worked out from the network's targets (`n_contacts`, `concurrency`, the duration, `acts`), with a note, a rule for networks with age mixing and several partnership types (19 B5), and `solvers()` reporting it. - **A report of the common-random-numbers contract** (review P7). It would take marking the draws that do not in `ss.Dist` and the networks, and printing them. Waits because nobody has needed it, and pairing is weak once runs diverge anyway (review R30).
- **A declared program (`ss.program()`).** Programs exist only from an Atomica program book (`read_progbook()` in `starsim/interop/atomica.py`): each becomes a spending parameter `spend_<program>` whose coverage moves the parameters it affects, which `ss.fit.optimize()` reallocates. Waits for a model outside Atomica that needs programs.

- **Exporting a stratification as separate species.** SBML and odin exports sum each stratification into one group (listed by `ss.LossyExport`). Writing each group as its own species (or an odin array) needs the mixing between groups, `adjust=` by group, and aging and other transfers written out from the engine's groups. It waits for someone who needs a stratified model in another tool.
- **Hosting the fetched contact matrices as release assets.** `starsim_data` fetches `prem2017.h5` and `mistry2021.h5` from GitHub at commit `56cb6a5` (`data/fetched.json`), each checked by sha256, so they stay reachable only while that commit is in the repository's history; a force-push or rewrite that drops it would break `fetch()` for both. Naming every one `recovery.dur`, with a unique short alias, would rename the parameters of most models (in `pars=`, `sim.fit()`'s tables, and the spec) for a modest gain in predictability. ## Extending a model with code (2026-10-25)

## Modeling paradigms (2026-10-23)

Looked at whether v4 should support modeling paradigms beyond the ODE, chain-binomial, Gillespie, tau-leaping, and agent solvers. Following [paradigm-conversion.md](best-practices/paradigm-conversion.md), each is treated as a point on an axis of the same declared model, not as a separate kind of model.

- **Branching processes (`solver='branching'`).** A branching process is the declared model with susceptibles never depleted (transmission linearized at the disease-free state): a new axis, depletion on or off, rather than new model text. What it would give that the current solvers don't: a cost that scales with the number infected rather than the population (people are created only when infected, so "the chance that one import into a city of 10 million takes off" can be run 100,000 times cheaply); chain-level outputs (chain size and length distributions, who infected whom by generation) for importation, elimination, and contact-tracing questions (Hellewell et al. 2020); and overdispersed offspring from heterogeneity agents already have (a gamma-distributed `rel_trans` gives negative binomial offspring). What it would take: agents created on infection with no population behind them; an expression that reads `S.n` held at its disease-free value, with a note, like the other standard approximations ([12-backends.md](design/12-backends.md#the-standard-approximations)); `'auto'` never choosing it. It also covers much of "different solvers for different transitions in one run" ([19-open-questions.md](design/19-open-questions.md)), whose usual reason is the stochastic tail of a disease near elimination. M. Proposed on 2026-10-23; not yet started.
- **The probability of a major outbreak (`dis.p_outbreak()` or `extinction_prob()`).** The fixed point of the multi-type offspring generating function, built on the next-generation matrix that `R0()` already computes (`starsim/diseases.py`, `engine.next_generation()`), with the offspring distribution each infected compartment implies (geometric for an exponential infectious period, more concentrated for Erlang stages). It would also give `ss.errors.ExtinctionPossible` a number to report. S; independent of the branching solver, and the cheaper of the two.
- **Renewal-equation models (EpiEstim, EpiNow2, epidemia), as the `'ide'` solver.** The mean-field counterpart of a branching process, and probably the most-used model type in outbreak analytics that v4 has no answer for. It is the same machinery as the deferred integro-differential solver ([12-backends.md](design/12-backends.md); [19-open-questions.md](design/19-open-questions.md)): an SEIR with arbitrary dwell times is a renewal equation with a particular generation-interval kernel. Framing `'ide'` as renewal would also give estimating Rt a natural home in fitting ([13-inference.md](design/13-inference.md)). L, and it widens v4's scope, so it waits for Cliff's decision on whether it comes out of deferred.
- **Examples of paradigms v4 already covers.** A model with no transmission is a cohort Markov model under `'binomial'` or `'ode'` and a microsimulation under `'abm'` (the health-economics and TLO audience, who won't otherwise see that it is covered); TSIR is `'binomial'` with `dt` equal to the generation interval. One sentence each in the spec and an example each in the corpus; no new code. S; waits with the rest of the docs until the implementation is final.

## Won't do

Work that was looked at and decided against, with why, so that it is not proposed again without a new reason.

- **camdl's `overdispersed`, `deterministic`, and `balance` as features, and more built-in likelihoods** (beta, beta-binomial, zero-inflated negative binomial, a normal whose spread depends on the model). Each is a keyword that one or two benchmark models need, and each is a few lines of code with what v4 already has: a connector that sets a value each step, a function of the sim, a module written as code, and a likelihood given as a function `fn(observed, model)`. Cliff (2026-10-25): no more complexity or built-in features, but a sensible, easy path for unanticipated extensions; that path is [tutorial G8](../docs/tutorials/g8_extending.qmd) and the tutorial `docs/tutorials/g8_extending.qmd`.

- **NumPy versions of the agent passes, to avoid their first-use compile.** An agent-based run compiles about 5 s of Numba passes (`census`, `count_alive`, `due_alive` and others) once per install; Cliff (2026-10-21): "5 s as a one-off compile is acceptable". So no second implementation of them (about 150–250 lines) for small populations.
- **Export to Petri nets.** A Petri net carries only the states and transitions (places and transitions), less than the Mermaid diagram does with its rates, and no importer reads one back to check the round trip. Decided by Cliff (2026-10-16).
- *Reversed: implemented in `66d865ef` ("Allow ss.null as one destination of a split") and tested (`tests/test_transitions.py`); kept here as history, not a reason to remove it (noted 2026-10-22).* **A branch into `ss.null` within one transition** (`ss.Transition(I, [R, ss.null], p=...)`). `ss.BadBranchSplit` says to write it as a transition of its own, each with `p=`, which works under every solver; allowing it in one would need each engine to take "outside" as one of a branch's destinations. Decided by Cliff (2026-10-16).
- **Export beyond Mermaid, SBML, and odin.** The code is the complete form of a model; exporting it to the other frameworks' formats would be lossy for each and needs a writer and a round-trip test per format, for little use. Decided by Cliff (2026-10-16); SBML and odin are kept only where they are little work, since their importers check the round trip.
- **Another step order for agents (item 18: options B, B′, and C; ST4).** Three conventions were built and measured so that agents would match the ODE at a coarse step (an exit chance r·dt/(1 + r·dt); that plus rates on counts after the start-of-step exits; every rate at the start of a step and every exit at its end); each fixed some models and broke others (spec 12 has the numbers). Cliff (2026-10-19): "just revert to the simplest, most natural interpretation in each case, and document how the different models give slightly different results ... if every method gave the exact same result for everything we wouldn't need to support different methods." ST4 (a dwell entered by transmission is counted one more step in results) is closed with it.
- **Aging from the edge density of a split (MA21).** In a compartmental run with a given age split, people age out of a group at one over its width; estimating the density at each edge from the neighboring groups holds a narrowing pyramid closer to agents (TVD 0.045 to 0.021 after 20 years) but departs from summer2 and textbook models, which age at one over the width (`test_summer2`: 135,866 against 106,893). Kept as the shared convention (decision 16: methods differ, and the others are for inspiration but their shared conventions win).
- **From the October 20 simplification reviews** ([oct20/simplify_plan.md](oct20/simplify_plan.md), "Won't do"): moving `ss.interop` or `ss.library` into separate packages (they stay, imported explicitly); JAX as an optional extra (it becomes a dependency); compatibility layers for removed names (v4 is unreleased; migration is by AI-assisted scripts; a removed name does now say what replaced it, from plan_b G2, which is a message, not a layer); dropping the stochastic compartmental solvers, or limiting declared agents to the core language; `Sim(modules=[...])` as the documented composition path; relative imports in place of `import starsim as ss`; plotting as a submodule or lazily imported; removing `ss.datedur`, merging `freq` into `per`, or moving `probper*`/`freqper*` and the time constants off the root; removing 2D results or `summarize_by`; moving `ss.Strains` or pregnancy out of core; an `ss.io` submodule. Decided by Cliff (2026-10-20).
- **An SDE (diffusion-approximation) solver.** The missing middle of the stochasticity axis (deterministic, diffusion, jump; [paradigm-conversion.md](best-practices/paradigm-conversion.md)). Its main use is POMP-style inference, which the particle filter on the chain-binomial solver already serves. Proposed for Won't do on 2026-10-23 in the paradigms review above.
- **Phylodynamic models (birth–death trees, the coalescent) and fitting to sequences.** A branching process with sampling is the birth–death phylodynamic model, so a branching solver with the infection log would give transmission trees (perhaps exported); fitting to sequence data belongs to BEAST and the like. Proposed for Won't do on 2026-10-23 in the paradigms review above.
- **Governance for the release** (from the October 22 skeptic review): a stability and deprecation policy for the declared language, and review of every pull request by someone other than its author. Decided by Cliff (2026-10-25). Likewise, the `/v4.review` reports need no baseline kept from earlier runs (no baggage).
- **A CRN-safe `ss.RandomNet`.** Keying each edge's random number on (source, stub) and finding its target among slots keeps 99.9–100% of edges when one agent dies (against 0–1% today), but every version was slower: 1.22–1.50× at 10k to 1M agents with no deaths, and 3.0–4.6× for the exact version with 30% of slots dead (2026-10-25, one thread). Cliff: only if it is no slower at all. `ssl.RandomSafeNet` is the CRN-safe network.
- **Settling the two-parameter NUTS coverage** (0.907 and 0.917 ± 0.011 against 0.95, after the trapped chains were fixed; exact grid posteriors under-cover by about 2.5 SE too). It would take about 2 core-hours (the grid posterior on all 400 replicates). Cliff (2026-10-19): low priority.

## Accuracy and features from the October 17 review

- **Conveyors for other dwell times.** A lognormal or Weibull dwell time with a short support could be a conveyor of whole steps releasing the distribution's discrete hazards (exact at the step) instead of Erlang stages (approximate in shape). It would change results for ordinary models, so it waits for Cliff.
- **A delay's history.** Starting a state with a fixed delay spread over its slots (as odin's `delay()` backfills its first days) would remove the extra inflow the odin vignette uses; it waits for a spelling.
- **Stays on transmissions, stays out of a state that splits on entry, and `ss.update` in diseases tracked jointly.** Each raises a `CapabilityError` today.
- **Exact exports of a delay.** SBML and odin exports write a conveyor as stages of one step each; odin's own `delay()` would be exact.
