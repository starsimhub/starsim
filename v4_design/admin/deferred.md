# Deferred

Work that has been looked at and set aside for now: worth doing, but not yet. Each item says what it would take and why it waits. Items that are done move to `implementation.md`.

## Performance

- **The rest of the deliveries in a whole run.** Deliveries on a schedule of a product people hold, or a transfer, to people chosen by states and stratifications, are taken by the kernels (`ss.Delivery.tabulated`). Still stepped from Python, at 120 to 300 µs a step against about 10 µs: tests and their follow-ups (a care cascade, as tbsim's `care()`), treatments that cure (moving people along a transition, credited to its flow), capacity, `when=`, deliveries in a model of several diseases, modifiers with `when=` or a function as their amount, and transfers whose rate varies. A screen whose follow-ups are all compiled could be one set of moves from the compartments at the time of the screen (each person's path through the cascade is a product of static shares): shifts between groups as now, and moves along a transition to its destination's entry compartments (with branches, maps between dimensions, and the flow credited), drawn as a multinomial under the stochastic solvers; a `when=` that is a function of the sim cannot be compiled. Not done yet (2026-10-16), pending Cliff's decision: the cascade is the larger piece, and would let tbsim's care cascade be fitted by NUTS.
- **Dropping the empty groups of a ragged stratification.** With `where=` on `stratify()`, a compartmental run keeps the full grid of groups: the people outside `where=` are counted at the first level, and the groups at the other levels stay empty but are still worked out (four age groups of women and one of men is eight groups, not five). Keeping only the groups that can hold people would need the group index to go through a lookup wherever it is now worked out by arithmetic (`group_mults`), in the solvers, kernels, movers, and fitting. Worth it only if a real model has many empty groups.
- **The rest of the gap to Ghana measles.** A chain-binomial run of Ghana measles (260 districts, 5,844 daily steps) takes 1.04 s, against 0.84 s for the original NumPy loop (measured 2026-10-16, after removing 0.7 s of overhead, and with the mixing between places done by `np.dot`). Of the 1.04 s: the random draws are 0.54 s (about 47 ns a binomial, the same in Numba, a Numba `np.random.Generator`, and NumPy, so there is little to gain there), the rest of the kernel (working out the rates, sharing out the events) about 0.2 s, and storing the results by district about 0.13 s. A hand-written Numba version of the original takes 0.72 s, which is the floor without running in parallel. What would close it: (1) results by group made from the flows only when asked for; (2) a kernel specialized to the model rather than interpreting its rates. Giving the last exit of a compartment the remainder rather than a draw saved only 3%, so was left out. Doing better than the original would need the groups drawn in parallel, with a random number stream per group so that runs stay reproducible.
- **The rest of an agent-based step.** After this round, what is left in a large agent-based step is spread out: the infection pass, exposure by place, and finding the members of each state (one `flatnonzero` a state), each 1 to 3 ms a step for 668,000 agents. Finding the members of every state in one pass gains little, since each state is its own array.

- **The particle filter on outcomes** (review W11). With an outcome such as `cases` as the data, `engine.collect(..., expected=True)` works out the whole run again at every data point, about 2.5 times slower than with a flow. It would take keeping the outcome's pending amounts between data points. It waits for the particles-in-the-kernel work (N27, phase 8), which changes the same code.
- **Threads in parallel workers.** NumPy's BLAS and Numba each start a pool of threads in every process. For one process this costs nothing (measured on the transition tests: the same CPU time, 1.5 to 1.7 times faster in wall time than with one thread), but many processes at once (pytest-xdist, `ss.parallel()`) oversubscribe the cores. It would take setting one thread in each worker (as `SCIRIS_NUM_THREADS=1` does for BLAS). It waits for a measurement of `ss.parallel()` with and without it.

## Accuracy

- **Thinning for time-varying rates under Gillespie.** Gillespie and tau-leaping hold a value that varies smoothly in time for a quarter of a step at a time (with a note), rather than thinning as the spec asks for. Thinning needs a bound on every rate over the step, which a rate written as any expression of the parameters does not give; a bound could be worked out by interval arithmetic over the kernels' operations, if exactness is needed.

## Features

- **Triangles as a target statistic.** `ss.PartnerNet` and `ss.MFNet` target the number of partners people have at a time and mixing by a property (`match=`). Triangles (two of a person's partners who are partners of each other), which an ERGM can also target, need the network fitted as a whole, not partner by partner.
- **WorldMove (synthetic movement within cities).** A collaborator suggested it ([Scientific Data, 2026](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41597-026-06555-2); https://github.com/tsinghua-fib-lab/WorldMove): one day of synthetic trajectories for each of about 1,600 cities in 179 countries, on a 1 km grid in half-hour slots, from a diffusion model, with commuting origin-destination matrices between the cells of each city. It is within cities, while `ss.geo` and `ss.gravity` are between administrative units, and a v4 run at a step of a day has no use for half-hourly paths. Its commuting matrices could give `ss.Mobility(matrix=...)` for a model of one city on a grid. It waits for a vignette that needs one; the repository has no license, and the paper is CC BY-NC-ND, so `starsim_data` could not redistribute it as things stand.
- **Gaps found while writing the importers and the import guides.** Each was worked around or listed as not translated. Fixed since (2026-10-18): `ss.PartnerNet(acts=)`, deaths from a life table (`ss.death(lifetable=)`), seeding a number of people on a schedule (a delivery with `move=` and `capacity=`), `ss.Mobility(scale='mean', cap=)`, the binomial solver's substeps for one Erlang stage (it now takes whole steps), and a one-off move between states (`ss.vaccinate(move='S -> V')`). Still open:
    - There is no declared condition on a network (a person's degree, or being the partner of someone infected).
    - A transition's current flow cannot be used in a rate (MEmilio's test-and-trace capacity): `I >> R @ (0.1 + 0.5*infection.incidence(over=ss.days(7)))` raises a `TypeError` ("condition has no single truth value").
    - The network of an `ss.contacts()` given as a matrix is written in the model spec as the mixing pools it makes, not as its matrices, so neither `ss.load()` nor `ss.to_code()` can rebuild it from the spec (an importer gives `to_code()` the code instead). A named one (`ss.contacts('Kenya')`) is written as where it came from, and is rebuilt.
    - `ss.PartnerNet` takes edges, concurrency, a cap on degree, matching on one attribute, and a number of partners by level (`n_contacts=ss.by(...)`, EpiModel's `nodefactor`); other ERGM terms (triangles, `nodematch(diff=TRUE)`) have no equivalent (EpiModel).
    - EpiModel lets people recover in the step they are infected, and counts after that, so its prevalence is 3 to 6% lower for the same epidemic; Starsim has no option for that order within a step.
    - An initial number below 1 is read as a fraction of the population, which a model with a fractional count (camdl's test models) cannot say otherwise.
    - Priors are not in the model spec, so `to_code()` lists them for adding by hand (likelihoods are now written).
    - In a compartmental run, a value that varies in time is taken at the start, middle, and end of each step, not at each substep, so a change within one step (MEmilio's one-day smoothing of a damping) is approximated, and `substeps=` does not help; a smaller `dt` does.
    - A count over the groups weighted by a parameter that differs between them (MEmilio's test-and-trace load, the sum over age groups of `(1 - mu)/T × INS`) cannot be written; `.n` is an unweighted total, so the importer uses the mean of the parameter.
    - A parameter of the infectious person's group (MEmilio's `RelativeTransmissionNoSymptoms` and `RiskOfInfectionFromSymptomatic` by age) can be given only as `rel_trans` for all infectious states together; a different one for each infectious state has no declared form.
- **`describe(verbose=True)`** (review P7, Decision docs 13, which recommends building it). It would show the per-step conversion of every converted quantity (`beta = 0.3 per day -> p = 1 - exp(-0.3 × 1) = 0.2592 per step`), the generated Erlang stages, and each schedule group's rows of the loop table, with a module moved by `sim.loop.insert()` at its new place (today the schedule ignores insertions). It would take a `verbose` argument on `Sim.describe()` and `Disease.describe()`, using the engine's dwell means and stage counts (`Engine.dwells`, `dwell_mean()`) and the loop's plan rather than the module list: about an hour with a test. It is simply not built yet; the spec no longer promises it.
- **The size of a stratified model, and its equations by group.** `describe()` lists each dimension's levels but not the number of compartments (groups times states), and `equations()` of a stratified disease prints the unstratified equations. The count is cheap (sum `sim.n_groups` times the states each dimension applies to, with a note above about 10,000 compartments, as the dropped `LargeStateSpace` warning was); an indexed form (`dS[a]/dt = -beta * S[a] * sum_b(C[a,b] * I[b] / N[b])`) needs the engine's groups and mixing written symbolically. Waits for a user who needs either: the sparse movers and factored mixing made large grids cheap.
- **The flow of each branch, or each source, of one transition.** A branching transition (`'I -> R | D'`) and one with several sources (`(S | I | R) >> D`) record only their total flow; the spec promised `.select(to=)` and `.select(source=)`. It would take the engines recording a flow per destination and per source (a `to` or `source` dimension on the result) in the kernels, the agents, and JAX. Waits because separate transitions with `p=` already give a flow per branch, and the extra dimension costs result memory for every branching model.
- **An outcome on the sim, across modules** (`ss.Sim([flu, rsv], ili=ss.outcome(0.3 * (flu.infection + rsv.infection)))`). The spec promised it; today `ili=` is an unknown parameter of the sim. It would take sim-level results built from several engines' flows by group, under every solver and in the kernels' whole runs and JAX, with data matching and likelihoods for a sim-level result. Waits because no vignette needs it: arithmetic on results after the run gives the series.
- **Fitting a product of parameters as one quantity** (`sim.calibrate(pars=[beta * contacts])`, which raises `ss.NothingToFit`). It would take `pars=` accepting an expression of `ss.par`s, with bounds from the factors, moving the first factor and holding the rest, and reporting the product. Waits because the `ss.Unidentifiable` note already says to fit one and fix the other.
- **A compartmental approximation of networks of lasting partnerships** (review X23). `ss.MFNet` and `ss.PartnerNet` under `ode`, `binomial`, or `gillespie` raise `ss.CapabilityError` ("no compartmental approximation yet"), although partner-change rates are the standard approximation for sexually transmitted infections. It would take partner-change rates between sex and risk groups worked out from the network's targets (`n_contacts`, `concurrency`, the duration, `acts`), with a note, a rule for networks with age mixing and several partnership types (19 B5), and `solvers()` reporting it. Waits because agents run these networks exactly, and the grouping for complex networks is open.
- **A report of the common-random-numbers contract** (review P7). The spec promised that `describe()` (or `sim.crn_report()`) would list which draws keep common random numbers (per-agent draws by slot) and which do not (sampling without replacement, joint draws). It would take marking the draws that do not in `ss.Dist` and the networks, and printing them. Waits because nobody has needed it, and pairing is weak once runs diverge anyway (review R30).
- **A declared program (`ss.program()`).** Programs exist only from an Atomica program book (`read_progbook()` in `starsim/interop/atomica.py`): each becomes a spending parameter `spend_<program>` whose coverage moves the parameters it affects, which `ss.optimize()` reallocates. It would take a Python object for a program's spending, unit cost, target population, and outcomes, built into the same parameters and modifiers the importer makes, and written by `to_code()`. Waits for a model outside Atomica that needs programs.

- **Exporting a stratification as separate species.** SBML and odin exports sum each stratification into one group (listed by `ss.LossyExport`). Writing each group as its own species (or an odin array) needs the mixing between groups, `adjust=` by group, and aging and other transfers written out from the engine's groups. It waits for someone who needs a stratified model in another tool.
- **Hosting the fetched contact matrices as release assets.** `starsim_data` fetches `prem2017.h5` and `mistry2021.h5` from GitHub at commit `56cb6a5` (`data/fetched.json`), each checked by sha256, so they stay reachable only while that commit is in the repository's history; a force-push or rewrite that drops it would break `fetch()` for both. Attaching the two files to a GitHub release and pointing the manifest at its URLs would take a two-line change. Deferred by Cliff (2026-10-16): the commit is on `main`, and nothing rewrites it.

- **Fitting: the scale of an all-zero data series** (review R6). It gets a scale of 1.0; the obvious fix makes its error always 1, so the series carries no information. Which scale it should have needs a choice.
- **The rest of the saved form** (review R29). Likelihoods and priors still go into the spec as text, and the `14-model-spec.md` grammar is not rewritten. Either changes hashes again, for models with likelihoods or priors only; priors in the hash is Decision results 13, which is not answered. It waits for phase 9 (the saved form).
- **The rest of the Atomica and camdl importers** (review N13). Atomica's junctions, interactions, and population types beyond the first, and camdl's interventions as deliveries, are listed by `ss.ImportIncomplete`. Atomica's programs are now read from a program book (`progbook=`), and a one-off move between states exists (`ss.vaccinate(move=...)`), which camdl's interventions can be written with.

- **Inline parameter names as `<transition>.<slot>`** (review L23, Decision lang 5). An inline value is named after its slot (`beta`, `dur`), qualified only on a clash (`dur_recovery`). Naming every one `recovery.dur`, with a unique short alias, would rename the parameters of most models (in `pars=`, `sim.calibrate()`'s tables, and the spec) for a modest gain in predictability. It waits for Cliff's view on Decision lang 5.
- **The rest of names without frames** (review L20, L21, L36, Decision lang 4). Done: one set of states and transitions can build several diseases (they are copied when another disease already has them), a string state name no longer picks up another disease's state from the caller, and a module that is not bound to one variable is named from its states (`sir`) rather than `disease`. Not done: dropping `ss.states(3)` for `ss.states('S I R')` (it would change most models and tests, and its failure, `ss.UnnamedState`, is already a clear error), and naming modules only structurally with no lookup at `ss.Sim` (it would name `hiv` and `syph` `si` and `si_1`). Both are for Cliff.

- **IF2 for observation and initial-value parameters.** `method='if2'` (built by N27) fits the model's own parameters. A parameter of how the data are observed (a reporting rate, a likelihood's dispersion) or one that sets the starting values is refused, pointing to `method='pmcmc'`: the first needs the likelihood evaluated with a value per particle, the second the starting compartments drawn per particle at the start of each pass.

## Tests

- **A test that triggers every named error, warning, and note** (review P6). The catalog in `design/15-errors.md` is generated and checked, but about twenty names are not triggered by name in any test, most of them notes and warnings: `AtBound`, `ClippedToRange`, `DataMisaligned`, `DuplicateDeclaration`, `ExtinctionPossible`, `LargeStep`, `MatrixAggregated`, `NotConverged`, `NothingToFit`, `OutcomeCycle`, `OverlappingSet`, `SmallCounts`, `SourceNeeded`, `StageStopped`, `StagesUnderAuto`, `StateNotPeople`, `TargetStatsNotRecovered`, `Unidentifiable`, `UnitInferred`, `UnknownDisease` (some are triggered by tests that match the message instead). It would take one parametrized test with a minimal trigger for each class, asserting the class and that the message names the user's identifier: about two hours. Waits for a quiet moment in the error register.
- **Golden `describe()` output for each corpus model** (review P10c). The expected text of each `tests/corpus.py` model's `describe()` in a YAML file, diffed and updated through the `baseline.yaml` and `update_baseline.py` pattern, so that a change in what a model means is reviewed. About an hour, plus reviewing 23 outputs. Waits because the text of `describe()` is still changing (guess marks, `describe(verbose=True)`).
- **A recorded table of solver differences for complex models** (`design/18-conformance.md`, suite 3). For the approximated corpus models (dwell times as stages, a network model as agents against its nearest ODE), the peak size, peak time, final size, and endemic prevalence under each solver, in a committed table regenerated on demand. About an hour. Waits for the solver menu and the kernels to settle, since every number would move.

## The model spec

- **Normalizing the order of operands** (`design/14-model-spec.md`). Sorting the terms of `+` and `*` when a rate is written for the spec would make `beta * I` and `I * beta` write and hash the same. It changes every hash once and needs care with nested expressions; a spurious difference is the safe direction, so it waits until hashes change anyway (R29).

## Won't do

Work that was looked at and decided against, with why, so that it is not proposed again without a new reason.

- **Export to Petri nets.** A Petri net carries only the states and transitions (places and transitions), less than the Mermaid diagram does with its rates, and no importer reads one back to check the round trip. Decided by Cliff (2026-10-16).
- **A branch into `ss.null` within one transition** (`ss.Transition(I, [R, ss.null], p=...)`). `ss.BadBranchSplit` says to write it as a transition of its own, each with `p=`, which works under every solver; allowing it in one would need each engine to take "outside" as one of a branch's destinations. Decided by Cliff (2026-10-16).
- **Export beyond Mermaid, SBML, and odin.** The model spec (`sim.to_spec()`) and `sim.to_code()` are the complete forms of a model; exporting it to the other frameworks' formats would be lossy for each and needs a writer and a round-trip test per format, for little use. Decided by Cliff (2026-10-16); SBML and odin are kept only where they are little work, since their importers check the round trip.

## A fittable `away=` (review of 2026-10-17, N20)

`ss.Mobility(away=ss.par(0.01, bounds=[...]))` raises a `TypeError` in `Mobility.time_share()`. Making it work needs the parameter registered with the disease that is stratified (as a modifier's amount now is), and the mixing between places worked out again when the value changes: today `sim.mixing` is built once at `init()`, and `sim.restart(pars=...)` (which fits use) would keep the old mixing. Either restart rebuilds the mixing and the engines' copies of it, or a fit of a mixing parameter runs a fresh sim at each point. Waits because it touches the mixing, which phase 5's one compartmental engine per sim (X25) rebuilds. The Ghana fit demo is blocked on it (and on N21).
- **Conveyors for other dwell times.** A lognormal or Weibull dwell time with a short support could be a conveyor of whole steps releasing the distribution's discrete hazards (exact at the step) instead of Erlang stages (approximate in shape). It would change results for ordinary models, so it waits for Cliff.
- **A delay's history.** Starting a state with a fixed delay spread over its slots (as odin's `delay()` backfills its first days) would remove the extra inflow the odin vignette uses; it waits for a spelling.
- **Stays on transmissions, stays out of a state that splits on entry, and `ss.update` in diseases tracked jointly.** Each raises a `CapabilityError` today.
- **Exact exports of a delay.** SBML and odin exports write a conveyor as stages of one step each; odin's own `delay()` would be exact.
