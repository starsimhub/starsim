## Semantics

3. **A plain duration on an outcome.** `ss.outcome(..., delay=ss.days(7))` shifts by exactly 7 days, but `duration=ss.days(10)` is an exponential stay (mean 10), as a duration on a transition is. flepiMoP means a fixed stay, and its `currHosp` was 15% off until the vignette wrote it another way. I've made `duration=` take a distribution too (`ss.constant(10, unit='days')` for a fixed stay), and left a plain duration exponential. Should a plain duration on an outcome be fixed, like `delay=`?
4. **`ss.modify(stop=...)` is exclusive.** flepiMoP's `period_end_date` is inclusive, which put one extra day of vaccination into a scenario. I've left `stop=` exclusive (`[start, stop)`, as `range()` is). Should there be an inclusive `end=` as well?
5. **Counts under the stochastic solvers.** The spec had a state that is not people (a reservoir, mosquitoes) drawn as whole numbers under `gillespie`, `tau`, and `binomial`. SimInf's SISe3 keeps its environmental pressure real (0 to 5), and with whole numbers it died out at once. I've made such states real-valued and deterministic under those solvers (people are still drawn), and changed `12-backends.md` and B2 to say so. A small mosquito population whose own randomness matters would now need an option on the state; is that acceptable?
6. **Adding days to a date.** `ss.date('2020-01-01') + ss.days(365)` gives 1 January 2021, not 31 December 2020 (and `+ ss.days(735)` gives 6 January 2022, not the 5th), so a day seems to be taken as 1/365.25 of a year. I left it alone, since it is Starsim 3's time system, but it surprised me when `stop=ss.days(735)` with a start date (which now works) ran one day longer than I expected.

## Design questions from wave 3 (not done)

7. **A fixed delay in a compartmental run (odin).** odin's `seir_array` has a latent period of exactly 14 days (`delay()`). Starsim makes `ss.constant` into Erlang stages, capped at 50 (undocumented): 6% off in `I` at 50 stages, 1.6% at 200, and the run time grows as the square of the stages (1.6 s at 200, against 2 ms for odin). A "conveyor" (a buffer of the cohorts that entered in each of the last 14 days, each thinned by deaths and aging), as spec 09 already does for an outcome's `delay=`, would be exact and cheap. It is a real change to the engines (a state with a fixed dwell would be a ring buffer rather than compartments). Worth doing?
8. **The history before the start (odin).** For t < 14, odin's delay uses the value at t = 0, so people are already arriving in `I` at the start, as if infections had been going on before it. `init=dict(E=14)` puts everyone in the first stage instead. An option to spread an initial count over a dwell time as if people had arrived at a constant rate (`init=dict(E=ss.spread(14))`?) would cover it.
9. **A count within a group, and a count over everyone (Atomica).** `.n` is local for a place dimension and the total otherwise. Atomica's rates need counts by sex, while program coverage needs the total over both sexes; the vignette makes sex a place (`kind='space'`) and gets the total from a function, which forces the run into Python. Proposal: `I.n` stays as it is, and `I.n.local` (or `I.n.within('sex')`) is the count in the person's own group of that dimension.
10. **Programs and budgets (Atomica).** Spending, unit cost, coverage, and effects on several parameters are written as expressions with `programs` and `budget` parameters. `ss.modify` can't target an expression inside a branch. Is a declared program form wanted (perhaps a delivery with `.coverage` usable in expressions), or are expressions enough?
11. **Minimum stays and "as soon as" (EMULSION).** EMULSION's `duration` holds every other exit back until it is over, and `escape` is allowed only until then. Proposal: `after=` and `before=` on a transition (a time in the state), plus a way to say "immediately when a condition holds" (now a rate of 1e9 with `where=`).
12. **Births on a transition (EMULSION).** One newborn per farrowing (not a Poisson number) needs a `step_state()` subclass. Proposal: `offspring=` on a transition, or a public `sim.people.add(...)` for custom code.
13. **Parameters drawn for each run (flepiMoP).** `prior=` is only used by calibration; flepiMoP draws `Ro` and the case-detection share from truncated normals for each run. Proposal: `ss.parallel(sim, n_runs=10, priors=True)` (or `sim.run(n_runs=..., draw=True)`) draws each `ss.par` that has a prior from the run's seed, records the values per run, and restarts where it can.
14. **A transition's flow in a rate (MEmilio).** MEmilio's test-and-trace threshold is on the number becoming symptomatic each day; the vignette rewrites it as `0.91/2*INS.n`, repeating parameters. Allow `symptoms.flow` (the transition's current rate) in expressions?
15. **Flows into a dimension the source lacks (summer2).** Done in the end: such a flow is recorded at the level people arrive at, under every solver. Say if you'd rather it weren't.
16. **New names.** I added `ss.truncnorm` (a normal between `low` and `high`), `ss.timeseries(mode='cosine', period=...)`, `where=` on transitions, comparisons in rates (`Q.n < 20`), `disease.diagram()` and `R0()`, `sim.R0()`, `msim.extinction()`, a `to_states` column in movement registers, `immunity=` on strains (in the spec already), `sim.groups_changed(uids)`, and the note codes `W503 ClippedToRange`, `W1201 SmallCounts`, and `W1202 ExtinctionPossible` (the last two in the spec already). Rename any you don't like.
17. **Conditions on networks (EpiModel).** PrEP eligibility in EpiModel's HIV example depends on the network (two or more partners across both layers, or an HIV+ partner); the vignette reads edge lists in a function. Proposal: lazy conditions such as `main.degree + casual.degree >= 2` and `ss.partner_of(hiv.infected)`.
18. **Acts per partnership (EpiModel).** `ss.PartnerNet` has no `acts=`, so infectiousness scales the chance per partnership rather than per act (about 1% here). Add `acts=`?
19. **Stopping a product that is held (EpiModel).** Stopping PrEP is written as `ss.Vaccine(waning=-1/log(1 - 0.015))`. A `stop=` (or `waning=ss.prob(...)`) on a held product would say it directly.
20. **The sim's copy of the model (odin).** `ss.Sim(seir)` works on a copy, so `seir.onset.dur = ...` after the sim exists silently does nothing to it (the vignette changes `sim.inputs['modules'][0]` instead). Should the sim say it copied the model, or use the object it was given?
21. **A posterior method.** epydemix's projections disagree with the original's (1,158 against 2,217 weekly deaths in one scenario) only because Starsim fits a point estimate where epydemix uses ABC-SMC. The spec says to wrap an existing implementation rather than write one, which means a new dependency (e.g. `pyabc`, or `pymc` for NUTS). I didn't add one without asking. Which, if any?
22. **Life-table deaths (LASER).** LASER draws each person's age at death once, from a life table, given their age; a hazard by age group gave 29.8k first-year deaths against 43.0k. The vignette writes `ppl.ti_dead` (not public). Proposal: `ss.death(age=ss.lifetable(...))` or `ss.People(lifespan=...)`.
23. **Scheduled importations (LASER, and SimInf's and Ghana's).** A fixed number of imported cases on a schedule needs a function that writes `S`, `I`, and `ti_I`. Proposal: `ss.seed('S -> I', n=3, interval=ss.days(14), stop=...)`, a delivery of infections.
24. **Gravity as LASER scales it.** LASER scales the coupling to a mean export share (0.0446) and caps each place at 0.2; `away=` sets every place to the same share. Proposal: `away=ss.mean(0.0446)` and `cap=0.2` on `ss.gravity`. Also, `ss.gravity`'s exponent on the origin is one more than LASER's (LASER's `a=0` is Starsim's `a=1`, since Starsim gives the share of each person's time); fine as long as the docs say so?
25. **`ss.gamma(40, 0.25)` is a mean of 40.25.** The second positional argument is `loc`, not the scale, so this silently shifts the distribution. It is Starsim 3's signature; make `loc` keyword-only in v4?
26. **Speed of the agent engine on LASER's model.** As agents, 110 ns an agent a step, about 45 times LASER at 668,000 people. I took 38% off (groups worked out again only for the agents that changed, and a state's members read from its array), so about 28 times now; the plan already says a whole agent-based run in one kernel is not planned. Is this gap acceptable for v4.0? One cost of the speedup: code that changes a property a dimension is made from (`people.patch`) now has to say so (`sim.groups_changed(uids)`), except for dimensions binned from a continuous property such as age, which are checked every step.
