# Best-practices folder: review

I read all 17 files in `v4_design/best-practices/` (3,900 lines), the design files they justify (`00`, `01`, `02`, `03`, `04`, `05`, `06`, `07`, `08`, `09`, `10`, `11`, `12`, `13`, `16`, `17`, `18`, `19`), `admin/implementation.md` and `admin/deferred.md`, and checked the claims I make below against `starsim/` with the v4 Python (scripts in the scratchpad; results quoted inline). Verdict: the arguments in the folder are mostly sound and the "Rejected" sections are the most valuable institutional memory in the repo, but the folder has become a second, staler copy of the design: every "The proposal" section restates a design file, and in at least eight places the practice, the design, and the code now say three different things. The two north-star tests are stated in four places and measured in none (no vignette table exists in this repo; the five timings scattered in `implementation.md` fail the time test in four cases), and principle 9 ("a feature that only pays off in one framework's use case does not go in") was abandoned in wave 3 without anyone saying so. The folder should be cut to its "Why", "Trade-offs", and "Rejected" sections and merged from 17 files to about 8.

## Top findings

1. **The two north-star tests and principle 9 pull in opposite directions, and the tests are not being measured.** `principles.md` §The test says each framework's examples must fit in "no more lines and no more time", and principle 9 says a feature that pays off in only one framework's use case does not go in. Wave 3 resolved the conflict in favor of the test: `ss.Mobility(kind='force')` (LASER only; `06-routes.md` §Commuting and migration), a register `to_states` column (SimInf's shift matrix), `ss.PartnerNet(match=)` (EpiModel's `nodematch`), `ss.truncnorm` (flepiMoP), `ss.timeseries(mode='cosine')` (MEmilio), `substeps=1` (epydemix), `'A+B'` strain levels and `move=` (tbsim), `where=` ragged stratification, `immunity=`, and `ss.Crossing` all went in to make one framework's held-out model agree. None of these is in `17-vocabulary.md`, which still claims 34 new `ss.` names; `dir(ss)` on the v4 branch has 285 public names, 71 of them absent from v3's `__init__.py` (and `ss.Expr`, listed under "Removed since the first draft", is exported). Meanwhile the measurement `18-conformance.md` §13 requires (one row per vignette: lines and time, both versions) exists nowhere in this repo: `v4_design/vignettes/` holds only a README, and `implementation.md` records lines for one framework (EpiModel, 62 against 405) and times for five, of which four fail the test (SimInf 3.0 s against 0.08 s; LASER England and Wales 131 s against 3.0 s; Ghana measles 1.04 s against 0.84 s; camdl NUTS 5.5 s against 2.3 s from the command line). Proposal: (a) publish the table, with the failing rows, as `18` §13 demands; (b) decide which of principle 9 and test 2 wins when they conflict, and say so in `principles.md`; (c) restate the time test honestly, for example "within a stated factor of a compiled original, and no slower than any Python original", because a Numba kernel will not beat SimInf's C Gillespie and pretending otherwise makes every row a "defect" nobody acts on.

2. **The folder is now a drifted duplicate of the design, and should be cut to the parts the design does not contain.** Every best-practices file has a "The proposal" section that restates the matching design file, usually with the same code. Where the two have drifted, the practice is wrong: `composition-and-effects.md` §Reading and writing a modified quantity ("reading gives the effective value", `.base`, the `*=` double-counting warning) is the opposite of the code (`starsim/arrays.py:915` `ModifiableArr`: reading gives the base, `.effective` gives the modified value; listed as a departure); `results-and-observation.md` and `17-vocabulary.md` say `I.prev`, the design and code say `I.frac` (`hasattr(I, 'prev')` is `False`); `calibration.md` §The method depends on the model and `13-inference.md` §Choosing the method make ABC-SMC the default for two of four rows, and no ABC exists (`starsim/calibration.py:2084` chooses `pmcmc`/`optuna`/`nelder-mead`/`nuts`); `paradigm-conversion.md` rec 5 and `12-backends.md` §Functions say "no static analysis, no declaration, try it", and code modules now declare `compartmental = True` (`starsim/connectors.py:210`, `products.py:277`) or are assumed agent-based, with the probe sim removed for modifiers ("they always work on groups", `implementation.md` §10 to 12); `metapopulation.md` lists `ss.radiation`/`ss.stouffer` as if present (one trailing note says deferred); `stochastic-compartmental.md` §Validity conditions promises leap-condition and `rate × dt` checks that were dropped (`W1203`, `W1204` unused). Proposal: strip every "The proposal" section and every "Lingua franca:" code block from the folder (the design owns that text), keep "Recommendation", "Why", "Trade-offs", "Rejected", and "Open questions", and merge: `compartmental.md` + `stochastic-compartmental.md` + `agent-based.md` + `paradigm-conversion.md` → one `solvers.md` (their non-duplicated content is the four "Why" sections and the approximation policy argument, perhaps 250 lines); `metapopulation.md` → absorbed into `stratification.md` (space as a dimension) and `population-and-mixing.md` (mobility); `principles.md` + `README.md` → one file. That leaves roughly `principles`, `model-structure`, `time-and-units`, `parameters`, `stratification`, `mixing`, `composition`, `interventions`, `results`, `reproducibility`, `calibration`, `solvers` at about 1,500 lines. What is lost: the side-by-side "camdl writes this, SimInf writes this" snippets, which are genuinely useful; move them into one `comparisons.md` appendix or into `approaches/`, where they belong by subject.

3. **`beta` changes meaning when a mixing pool is added, and the change is a factor of `n_contacts`.** `population-and-mixing.md` §What the user writes and `06-routes.md` §What a state in a rate means: with no network `beta` is a rate per unit time; with `ss.MixingPool(n_contacts=10)` the same number is per contact. Verified: the same `ss.transmission('S -> I', beta=0.3)` peaks at 3,036 infected with no network and 8,514 with `networks=ss.MixingPool(n_contacts=10)`. The docs call this "one definition, not two", which is true mathematically and false for the user, who adds `n_contacts=10` to say "people have ten contacts a day" and gets an epidemic ten times as fierce. The trade-off is acknowledged (`population-and-mixing.md` §Trade-offs, first bullet) but no mitigation beyond `describe()` is given. Proposal: print `beta` with its resolved units on the solver line at run time (`beta = 0.3 per contact × 10 contacts/day = 3.0/day`), and print `W` when a bare `beta` is combined with an `n_contacts` other than 1, since a bare number was almost certainly typed with the textbook meaning. Cheaper still: have `describe()` show the resolved hazard `beta × c` as one number, which makes the ×10 visible at a glance.

4. **The unit-inference ladder guesses in cases where an error is cheaper, and two unit regimes coexist in one sim.** `time-and-units.md` rec 4 and `02-types.md` §The sim's unit: `unit=`, then `dt`, then dates, then typed values, "smallest with a note" if they disagree. Verified: `ss.Sim(sir, stop=ss.weeks(12))` with `beta=ss.perday(0.3)` and `dur=ss.days(10)` runs in weeks (`sim.unit == 'week'`, 13 points), because `stop`'s unit outranks the model's typed values; the known-issues list records this. And a v3 code module in the same sim keeps v3's convention (`ss.SIS(beta=0.1)` next to a declared disease prints "beta ... is read in years ... but the sim runs in days"; `implementation.md` §Departures, first bullet). So the practice "a bare number takes the sim's unit" is true for declared models and false for every module in `starsim/library/` and every user's v3 subclass. Proposal: (a) take the alternative already written in `19-open-questions.md` B1 ("an error asking for `unit=`") whenever typed values disagree with each other or with `dt`/`stop`; (b) make the v3 warning an error in the same case, or convert v3 library modules to read bare numbers in the sim's unit when the sim has one. The practice should not promise a uniform rule the library cannot keep.

5. **The string-form source-of-infection guess is the one guess the folder admits cannot be checked, and it should not be made.** `model-structure.md` §The source of infection and `03-states-and-transitions.md` §Source inference: "there is no way to tell [SIR and SEIR] apart from the structure", so the guess is only printed. Verified: `ss.transmission('S -> E', beta=0.3)` with `E -> I -> R` builds a model where `E` infects, marked `[guessed: source is the destination]` and nothing louder. `principles.md` §Guessing defaults sets the rule "an error is a claim that no guess is safe", and this guess is unsafe exactly when the model has a latent state, which is the second model anyone writes. Proposal: guess the destination only when the disease has at most one state downstream of the destination that is not absorbing (SIR, SIS, SIRS); otherwise require `source=` with an error naming the candidates. Cost: one keyword in the SEIR string form, which the docs already tell people to write.

6. **Initial conditions have three spellings and a magic-number rule.** `03-states-and-transitions.md` §Initial conditions: `ss.State(init=)`, `init_prev=`, `init=dict(...)`; "a value below 1 is a fraction, above 1 a count; a Python `int` of exactly 1 is one individual and a `float` of exactly 1.0 is everyone". Verified: `init=dict(I=1)` gives `n_I[0] = 1`, `init=dict(I=1.0)` gives `1000`. A rule that turns on `int` versus `float` for the value 1 is the kind of thing `principles.md` says to stop on ("a guess would be wrong quietly"), and it breaks under `ss.by`, data files, and JSON round trips where the type is not preserved. Proposal: `init=` is always a fraction; a count is written as one (`init=dict(I=ss.n(50))` or `init_n=dict(I=50)`); keep `init_prev=` only as the Starsim 3 compatibility name. `19` B1 already lists "separate keywords" as the alternative; take it.

7. **The calibration practice is the one most out of step with the code, and its default rests on a habit.** `calibration.md` rec 2 chooses the normalized absolute error "because it is Covasim's default", which is a framework-habit argument of the kind the folder criticizes elsewhere; an experienced calibrator would object that for count data it weights a 10-case miss at the tail and at the peak equally and misbehaves on zeros (the doc concedes both). Rec 3's table and `13-inference.md` §Choosing the method promise ABC-SMC for stochastic compartmental and cheap agent-based models; the code has no ABC (departure), fits agent-based sims with Optuna, and gets posteriors only with a likelihood. `sim.sensitivity()` is specified in both files and in `17-vocabulary.md`, is not in the code (no `def sensitivity` anywhere in `starsim/`), and is not in `deferred.md`. Staged fitting (`method=[...]`) is "not yet". Proposal: rewrite the method table to what exists (point estimate without a likelihood; posterior with one; `pmcmc` for stochastic compartmental, `nuts` for ODE, Optuna otherwise); make the default goodness of fit depend on the result's type (Poisson deviance for a count, NAE for a fraction), which needs no noise parameter and is not thrown off by zeros; add `sensitivity()` to `deferred.md` or build it, since LHS over bounds is two lines on top of `ss.parallel(pars=)`.

8. **Six constructors, two names, and two list keywords say one thing each.** (a) `ss.change(target, at=, by=)` is exactly `ss.modify(target, multiply=ss.timeseries({...}, mode='constant'))`: verified, maximum difference 0.0 over 60 steps. `interventions.md` rec 5 argues for it from Covasim's `change_beta`, which is nostalgia, not evidence. (b) `sim.results.sir.infection` and `sim.results.sir.new_infections` are one series under two names (`describe()` prints "and one flow per transition; also new_infections, prevalence"); `19` B1 says "to be tried both ways in the vignettes" and 30 vignettes later it is still both. (c) `ss.transmission`, `ss.progression`, `ss.birth`, `ss.death`, `ss.immigration`, `ss.emigration`, `ss.transfer` are seven lowercase constructors for one `ss.Transition`, where `03` itself shows `ss.death('I ->', rate=r)` is `ss.Transition('I', ss.null, rate=r)`. (d) `eligible=` on deliveries and `where=` on modifiers and transitions are the same concept ("who") under two keywords. Proposal: drop `ss.change` (keep `ss.modify(..., multiply=ss.timeseries(...))`, and teach it first); pick one flow name (`new_<transition>` reads better and matches Starsim 3) and alias the other for one release; fold `ss.immigration`/`ss.emigration` into `ss.birth`/`ss.death` keywords (`ss.birth('-> S', rate=r, ages='population')`); rename `eligible=` to `where=`. Each removal is one fewer thing in the table of slots in `17`.

9. **"Approximate rather than refuse" (principle 8) has become "implement every feature twice", and the folder should say what that costs.** `principles.md` rejects "a separate specification for each paradigm" (MEmilio's families). What was built instead is one specification with two realizations of nearly every feature, each a special case: products become a dimension in a compartmental run, waning becomes 10 levels of time since delivery, a test delay becomes `pending_<delivery>` levels, a condition on another disease's state becomes `ss.Crossing` with its own "not yet" list, strain history keeps only the last strain, ragged `where=` keeps empty grid cells (`implementation.md` §05 to 08). `12-backends.md` §The standard approximations is the honest table, and it is the thing the best-practices folder never argues for or against: a user who writes `ss.vaccinate(bcg, waning=fn)` on an ODE model gets a model with ten extra compartments per state, noted in one line. The practice should state the budget: when the compartmental realization multiplies the state space by more than some factor (`W309` exists for the total), `'auto'` should prefer agents rather than build it, and `approx=False` should refuse. That is a stronger version of the dwell-time cap already open in `19` B3.

10. **Names from the caller's variables are a convenience the folder itself calls "slightly magical", and they leak into results and data.** `model-structure.md` §Open questions and `01-objects.md` §Names: a module is named from the variable it is bound to. In my own test, `sir3 = ss.Disease(...)` produced `sim.results.sir3`, so renaming a Python variable renames a results key and the data column it must match (`09-results.md` §Data: columns are matched to result names). For states there is no fallback and an error (`E210`) when the lookup fails in a loop, a comprehension, or a REPL. Proposal: keep the lookup for transitions (it has a safe fallback) and modules (Starsim 3 already names from the class), but make `ss.states('S I R')` the documented form and `ss.states(3)` the shortcut, and have `describe()` print `[named from variable]` so the dependence is visible. This is a smaller vocabulary, not a larger one: one fewer rule to explain.

## Other findings

- **`compartmental = True` is the declared rank the practice rejected.** `paradigm-conversion.md` rec 5 and `19` A ("Try it and report. No static analysis, no declared rank"). The departure is listed, but the practice document still argues for the opposite; rewrite it as "functions are tried; modules written as code declare".
- **`set=` on `ss.modify` is the rejected `Overwrite` under another name.** `stratification.md` §Adjustments are multiplicative only rejects `Overwrite` because it does not commute and depends on order; `07-effects.md` accepts `set=` with the later-in-loop-wins rule and `W502`. Either the argument against `Overwrite` is wrong or `set=` should be documented as the exception it is (needed for `ss.change(to=)`).
- **`select(age=0)` by position contradicts "names, always, checked".** `principles.md` table rejects positional references; `09-results.md` §Selecting strata allows `select(age=0)` "by position or by label", which is ambiguous for a dimension with a level labeled `0`. Allow labels only.
- **Counts are deterministic under every stochastic solver** (known issue), so `stochastic-compartmental.md` rec 5 ("a vector population is a state holding a count, with its own transitions") is only half delivered: a small mosquito population cannot go extinct. `agent-based.md` §Trade-offs says "which one was used should be printed"; nothing prints it.
- **The lazy short form needs a stub object the paradigm does not have.** `interventions.md` §Eligibility sells `(ppl.age > 65)` as "nothing new to learn"; in a compartmental sim it needs `ppl = ss.People(1)` to exist (known issue), while the "general form" lambda works without it (verified both). Until that is fixed the lambda, not the lazy form, is the one to teach for compartmental models.
- **59 error codes are specified, 42 exist in code**, and `18-conformance.md` §11 says a code without a test is removed. `15-errors.md` should be regenerated from the code.
- **`implementation.md` contradicts itself on `ss.geo`**: §Departures says "there are no named geographies", §05 says 184 countries are bundled. Delete the stale departure.
- **Stale cross-references in the folder**: `results-and-observation.md` rec 4 says outcomes "chain" and `duration=` gives prevalence; the code makes `duration=` an exponential stay (departure), which moves an outcome closer to a state and strengthens `19` B8's doubt about whether `ss.outcome` is needed. `time-and-units.md` §Trade-offs proposes a `describe()` schedule view; built. `stochasticity-and-reproducibility.md` promises a CRN audit in `describe()` (`✓ infection ... ! network.rewire`); nothing in `sim.describe()` (`sim.py:1076`) prints it, and `crn_report` does not exist. Mark both as not built or drop them.
- **Two keywords for modifiers.** `07-effects.md`: a modifier goes in `connectors=` or `interventions=`, "differing only in where the condition is evaluated". The code also hashes them differently (`sim.py:1315`: only connectors enter `model`; interventions enter `scenario`), which is the real reason for two lists and the one worth writing down; the loop-position difference is O(dt) and not a reason.
- **The `ss.freq` family (4 names) is used for `acts=` and nothing else in the folder**; `19` B1 proposes requiring it on inflows, which would give it a job. Either do that or drop it from the taught set in `17`.
- **Framework-habit arguments**, beyond the two above: `agent-based.md` rec 7 and §Population (preallocation "benchmark before building"; fine) and `composition-and-effects.md`'s claim that queued updates "put an artificial delay of two timesteps" (true of `individual`, but one step is the usual cost, and the sentence overstates it).
- **The AI-native section of `principles.md` has no status.** Items 1 (offline, doc-tested docs) and 2 (a CLI or `AGENT.md` contract; "both chose a CLI over an API") are not in the design, the implementation, or `deferred.md`; `skills/` exists and is not mentioned in the folder. Either track them or drop the section.
- **Missing practices.** Nothing in the folder covers demographics as a modeling concern (life tables, age-specific fertility, population growth, how `total_pop` evolves; `deferred.md` notes life-table deaths have no declared form); seeding a fixed number of cases on a schedule, and one-off moves between states at a date (camdl's `transfer`), which are among the most common things a modeler needs and have no declared form; parameter uncertainty propagation (`ss.parallel(sim, pars=...)` is built, undocumented as a practice); model checking beyond `R0()` (conservation of `N`, a published `describe()` baseline per model is in `18` §9 but not argued for); and co-infection in a compartmental run, which the composition doc claims "works if the condition is a state" and the code delivers through `ss.Crossing` with a growing exclusions list.
- **Redundancy within the folder** (same argument, several files): the cholera and malaria examples appear in `model-structure`, `population-and-mixing`, `stochastic-compartmental`, `agent-based`, and (as wastewater) `metapopulation`; the states-that-are-not-people inference rule in five files plus `stratification` open questions; the `solver='auto'` and Erlang policy in seven; the ordered-loop argument in `composition-and-effects` and `agent-based`; the three spellings in `principles`, `model-structure`, `agent-based`; capitalization in `model-structure` and `agent-based`; the school-closure modifier in three; `sim.hash()` in three; vaccination-as-transfer in two. The merge in finding 2 removes all of these.

## Things that are good

- One transition class whose kind is read from its rate, `ss.null`, and the inference of which states are people (`model-structure.md`): this is the idea the whole design rests on, every held-out model used it, and it should not be touched.
- `describe()` with `[guessed]` marks, and the rule "an error is a claim that no guess is safe" (`principles.md`): keep, and apply it more strictly (findings 5 and 6).
- Data attached by result name and `sim.calibrate()` with nothing but `bounds=` and `data=` (`calibration.md` §The whole interface; the 30-lines-to-3 comparison in `13-inference.md` is the best single argument in the design).
- `ss.modify` with a base that is never overwritten; `stratify` in place with `inplace=False`; `approx=` as a tri-state; the ordered loop with deferred removals and joint competing hazards; per-distribution streams and slot-keyed CRN. All argued well and all built.
- The "Rejected" sections. They are the only record of why `ss.Compartments`, `n_reps`, `ss.Homogeneous`, `min_duration=`, global predicate names, and the dict-tuple shorthand went away, and they are what stops them coming back. Whatever is merged, keep every "Rejected" bullet.
- `17-vocabulary.md` §Adding to the vocabulary, the five-question checklist. It is right; it was not applied in wave 3 (finding 1).

## Questions for Cliff

- Is `best-practices/` meant to remain a document people read, or to become the design's justification appendix? The merge in finding 2 assumes the second.
- When principle 9 and the north-star line-count test conflict (finding 1), which wins? If the test wins, say so and delete principle 9; if principle 9 wins, several wave-3 features belong in `starsim/library/` or a `starsim_interop` package rather than `ss.`.
- Should the time test be restated (within a factor of a compiled original) or held to as written, with SimInf and LASER rows published as design defects?
- Default goodness of fit: keep NAE, or choose by result type (finding 7)?
- Drop `ss.change`, and pick one of `infection`/`new_infections` (finding 8)?
- Drop the `int` 1 versus `float` 1.0 rule for `init` (finding 6)?
- Should `'auto'` refuse to build a compartmental realization past some state-space multiplier and go to agents instead (finding 9)?
