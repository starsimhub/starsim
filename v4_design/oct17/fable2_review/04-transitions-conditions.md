# Transitions and conditions: review

I read `v4_design/design/03-states-and-transitions.md`, `04-parameters.md`, `05-dimensions.md`, `07-effects.md`, `17-vocabulary.md`, `admin/implementation.md`, `admin/deferred.md`, then `starsim/transitions.py` (2,972 lines), `starsim/conditions.py` (446), the `ss.Disease` half of `starsim/diseases.py`, the expression contexts in `solvers.py`, `gradients.py`, `kernels.py` and `spec.py`, and `tests/test_transitions.py` / `test_dimensions.py`; I ran about sixty small expressions in the v4 python to check the operator form (scripts in the scratchpad: `ops.py`, `ops2.py`, `ops3.py`). The verdict: the user-facing language is in good shape and the operator form behaves exactly as the spec says in every case I tried, including the precedence tricks; the `Expr` tree with one `evaluate()` and pluggable contexts is the right design and should not be touched. The cost is on the maintainer side and at the seams: there are two expression languages (`Expr` for rates, `Cond` for who) that do not compose and crash unhelpfully when mixed; `Transition` carries about a dozen attributes that exist for one helper or one consumer each; `transfer` is a third kind of thing that is really `Transition(move=)`; and the file is four of the spec's chapters in one place. A 1,500-line `transitions.py` would lose nothing a user can see.

## Top findings

1. **Rates and conditions are two expression languages, and nothing guards the seam.** A rate is an `Expr` tree (`transitions.py:96-423`): `Q.n < 20` is a `Func('less')` that evaluates to 1 or 0. A condition is a `Cond` tree (`conditions.py:26-115`): `ppl.age > 65` and `sir.I & ~sir.R` are `Cond` nodes with their own `eval(ctx)`, their own `__bool__` guard, their own repr, and their own copies of `+ - * / > < == &  | ~`. The two are built by the same operators on different objects and cannot be mixed: `ss.Transition(I, Q, rate=0.1, where=Q.n < 20)` fails with `TypeError: bad operand type for unary ~: 'Func'` (from `sim.py:311`, because `as_cond` at `conditions.py:182-195` returns a `Func` unchanged), and `S >> I @ 0.3 * I * (ppl.age > 5)` is accepted silently (`as_expr` at `transitions.py:93` wraps the `Cond` in `par(literal=True)`) and dies at run time with `float() argument must be ... not 'Cond'`. The spec's own examples put the same comparison on both sides of the line (`I >> Q @ 0.5 * (Q.n < 20)` in 03 §Counts; `where=ppl.age > 1` in the same paragraph), so users will cross it. Proposal: one tree. `Cond`'s four leaves (`people`, `state`, `dim`, `array`) become `Expr` leaves alongside `State`, `Count`, `Flow`, and `par`; `&`, `|`, `~` become `Func`s (`State.__and__` already delegates to `Cond`, `transitions.py:493-500`); `Cond.eval` becomes `evaluate()` with `Selector` as one more context (it already has the same `leaf`/`value` shape). `conditions.py` keeps `Selector`, `is_static`, and `state_refs`; the ~190 lines of `Cond`, `evaluate`, `as_cond`, `as_bool`, `lazy`, `from_arr` go. If that is too large a change for now, the minimum is a guard: `as_expr` raises on a `Cond` and `as_cond` accepts a `Func` comparison of counts, each with a message that names the other form. What it breaks: nothing a user writes; `ss.Cond` is public (`__init__.py:130`) but appears in no test or doc outside `test_dimensions.py:260`'s `ss.Strat`.

2. **`0.5 * infection` means two things, and `infection / 2` a third.** `Transition._arith` (`transitions.py:739-761`) does both jobs on `*`: it rewrites the rate (`new.rate = 0.5 * rate`) *and* records `flow_of = (infection, 0.5)` and clears the name. So `ss.outcome(0.5 * infection)` reads the weight (spec 17 §Removed: "A number times a flow already means that fraction of it"), while `ss.Disease(half=0.5 * infection, ...)` builds a transition at half the rate (spec 03: "`infection * 0.5` is the same transition at half the rate") whose coefficient 0.5 is then registered as a parameter called `beta_half` (I checked: `half.rate` prints `beta_half * beta_infection * I`), and `ss.Disease(2 * infection, recovery)` positionally loses its name and becomes `S_to_I`. Meanwhile `infection / 2` is neither: it returns a `BinOp` of `Flow` objects for a result (`incidence = infection / S`, 09), and its repr is `None / 2` until the transition is named. One object, three meanings decided by the slot it lands in. Proposal: arithmetic on a finished transition is always a flow weight and never a rate change, returning a small `Weighted(flow, w)` (what `FlowSum` already holds) rather than a copied `Transition`. Halving a rate is written where the rate is, or with `ss.modify`. Delete the "same transition at half the rate" sentence from 03 and the `new.rate` rewrite from `_arith`; `Expr.__mul__`'s special case for `Transition`/`outcome`/`FlowSum` (`transitions.py:127`) then goes too.

```python
# before: both of these are legal and mean different things
half  = 0.5 * infection                       # a transition at half the rate, if passed to ss.Disease
cases = ss.outcome(0.5 * infection)           # half the flow, if passed to ss.outcome

# after: one meaning
cases  = ss.outcome(0.5 * infection)          # half the flow
slower = S >> I @ 0.5 * beta * I              # half the rate is written as a rate
```

3. **`Transition` is a bag of 22 attributes, most of them for one helper or one consumer.** After `__init__` and `Graph` it holds `src dst rate dur p stages name label` (the spec's fields), plus `beta source` (only `ss.transmission`, consumed and set to `None` at `transitions.py:1328-1329`), `per_capita` (only `ss.birth`, `1332`), `every_state` (only `ss.death()` with no arrow, `1376`), `migration` (only `immigration`/`emigration`, read at `solvers.py:3492,3696`), `flow_of` (only outcomes, finding 2), `where`, `move`, and the Graph-assigned `const terms contact people module guessed`. Because the private pre-resolution fields are constructor keywords, `ss.Transition('S', 'I', beta=0.3)` is accepted and prints `S -> I @ par(0.3) * source`, although 17 lists `ss.Transition`'s keywords as `rate dur p stages name label`. Proposal: (a) `transmission()` is the one helper that genuinely needs deferral (the source is the infectious states, known only at build); keep `beta`/`source` but as a single private `pending` attribute, not keywords; (b) `birth()` and `death()` do not need flags: `birth` can multiply by a `Count(everyone=True)` leaf now (the `kind='N'` sentinel at `1333`/`1381` is the same deferral in disguise), and `death()` with no arrow can set `src=ss.everyone` (a second sentinel like `ss.null`) that `resolve_people` expands; (c) `migration` is one boolean on two helpers, see "Other findings"; (d) `flow_of` leaves with finding 2. The class hierarchy as it is, and as it could be:


4. **`ss.transfer` is `Transition(move=)` under another name, is not a `Transition` although the spec says it is, lacks the `p=` the spec gives it, and returns two unrelated types.** Spec 05 §`ss.transfer`: "A transfer is an `ss.Transition`" and "`p=` and `dur=` are accepted as for any transition". Code: `class Transfer` (`transitions.py:2896-2935`) is a plain object; `ss.transfer(v=True, p=0.5)` raises "takes one dimension ... not ['v', 'p']" and `ss.transfer(v=True)` raises "takes exactly one of rate= or dur=" (`2918`); `ss.transfer(v=True, coverage=0.5)` returns an `ss.Delivery`, which is an `Intervention` and a `Module` (`2961-2967`). None of this is in the departures list. But `Transition` already has `move=` (`652-676`): "a change of level in a dimension as well as of state", with an arrow form that also restricts who moves (it sets `where`). A transfer is exactly a transition whose state does not change and whose `move=` does: `ss.transfer(risk='low -> high', rate=r, states=[I])` is `ss.Transition(I, I, rate=r, move=dict(risk='low -> high'))`, and the default of "every state" is the same `every_state` expansion `ss.death()` uses. Proposal: `Transfer` becomes a `Transition` built that way (keeping the `ss.transfer` spelling), which gives it `p=`, `stages=`, `where=`, the flow result, `modify` targeting, and the engines' existing `move` path for free, and deletes the separate `transfers` container in `Graph` (`1135, 1171-1173, 1434, 1841-1851`) and its special cases in the engines. Whether `ss.transfer(coverage=...)` should keep returning a `Delivery` is a question for Cliff (below); a function that returns a `Module` or a `Transition` depending on which keywords are present is the kind of thing 17's "Adding to the vocabulary" warns against.

5. **The addressability the spec promises (`sir.infection.beta`) is broken, and `rate` and `dur` are asymmetric after build.** 04 §The default: "Both values are addressable through their transition, as `sir.infection.beta` and `sir.recovery.dur`, for modifiers". In the code `tr.beta` is consumed into `tr.rate` and set to `None` (`transitions.py:1329`), so `sir.infection.beta` is `None` and `ss.modify(sir.infection.beta, ...)` raises `E504 ... not None (NoneType)`. The slot *is* known: `resolve_pars` labels the coefficient `'beta'` at `1412-1415`. Separately, `Transition.dur` is a property whose setter writes into the registered parameter after build (`716-728`, so `sir.recovery.dur = 5` works and updates `sir.pars.dur`), while `rate` is a plain attribute: `sir.infection.rate = 0.1` is accepted and the run then fails with `AttributeError: 'float' object has no attribute 'leaves'`. Proposal: keep `tr.beta` pointing at the registered `par` (one line in `resolve_pars`), and either give `rate` the same setter or, simpler, drop the `dur` magic and say once that values are set through `sir.pars` / `sir.set_pars()`; the spec's `sir.recovery.dur` target still works since it is the same `par` object. Add the departure to `implementation.md` if the spec sentence stays.

6. **`|` and `+` on states have become synonyms; one of them, and `StateSet`, can go.** 03 §The operators lists both "`S | I | R`: a set of states" and "`S + I + R`: a sum of states". The implementation makes them interchangeable: `(Ia + Is) >> R` works via `BinOp.__rshift__` (`transitions.py:318-320`), `(Ia | Is)` in a rate or with `.n` is converted to the sum (`542-565`), and a model written with `|` and one written with `+` have the same model hash (checked). `StateSet` (`518-566`) exists only to carry `|`, and it re-implements eight arithmetic dunders to forward to `sum()`. Proposal: `State.__or__` returns `self + other`, delete `StateSet`, and in `as_cond` treat a `+` of states as "in any of them". The spec table loses one row and the user loses nothing (the parentheses rule is the same, since `|` and `+` both bind looser than `>>`). The only reason to keep `|` is that it reads as "or" in a condition (`where=sir.Ia | sir.Is`); as a synonym it still does.

7. **Name capture is worth keeping; its sharp edges are few and local.** The lookup (`find_name` `1075-1084`, `resolve_states` `1246-1260`, `resolve_pars` `1420-1445`, `Disease.__init__`'s `sys._getframe(1)` at `diseases.py:74`) is about 60 lines and worked in every form I tried: `ss.states(3)` in a function, class bodies via `__set_name__`, a factory called with `**kwargs` whose states are module globals, mixed objects and strings. The edges: a state bound to two names is `E210` (as specified); a `Disease` subclass whose own `__init__` calls `super().__init__(*transitions)` looks in the subclass's frame, not its caller's, so `Wrap(S >> I @ 0.3*I, ...)` works when `S, I, R` are module globals (the lookup falls through to `f_globals`) and fails `E210` when they are locals of the calling function (checked both); `2 * infection` passed positionally is renamed `S_to_I` (finding 2); and `resolve_pars` names the *leftmost factor* of a contact rate as the beta (`1410-1412`), so `0.5 * infection` registers `beta_half = 0.5`, and `S >> I @ R0 * 0.1 * I` with a named `R0` registers the literal 0.1 as a parameter called `rate` while `R0` gets no slot (checked: `pars = {rate: 0.1, R0: 2.0}`). Deferred-resolution is the right price for `S, I, R = ss.states(3)`; the explicit `ss.states('S I R')` fallback already exists and the error says so. Two cheap improvements: walk up from `_getframe(1)` to the first frame outside `starsim/` (so subclasses work), and when a transmission's rate is `a * b * I`, name the whole coefficient of `I` the beta rather than its leftmost factor.

8. **There is one expression language, evaluated two ways and printed two ways; the smallest `Expr` is the one that exists, minus two sentinels.** `evaluate(expr, ctx)` (`transitions.py:395-423`) is the single tree-walker, and it serves the compartmental engine, the agent engine (`solvers.py:1201`, `3451`), results (`1540-1565`), constants (`Static`, `2217`), and JAX (`gradients.py:762-795`, which swaps `ctx.xp` for `jax.numpy`). That is one language, not three; the Numba kernels need a second evaluator (`flatten` into opcodes, `solvers.py:2147-2185` → `kernels.py:21-23`) and `spec.Parser` (`spec.py:62-103`) reads strings back in, both unavoidable. The duplication is in printing: every node has a `to_str` (`226, 264, 280, 304, 326`) and `format_expr` (`1927-1952`) walks the same tree again with the same `join_binop`, differing only in `values=` and `bare=`; `Cond.__repr__` is a third printer. And two leaves are overloaded: `par` is both a named parameter and an anonymous literal (`literal=True`, `93`), so `.literal` and `is_one` are tested 14 times across `linear`, `resolve_pars`, `format_expr`, `outcome.to_json`; and `Count(kind='N')` is a placeholder for "everyone" filled in later (`1333, 1381`). Proposal: `to_str` calls `format_expr`; add a `Const` leaf for literals and a `Count(everyone=True)` for N. The undocumented context protocol (`value`, `local_count`, `local_frac`, `exposure`, `flow`, `xp`) deserves one docstring on `evaluate`, since five classes implement it.

9. **`transitions.py` is four of the spec's chapters in one file; splitting it along those lines gives a ~1,500-line core with nothing lost.** By section: errors and notes `29-80` (50); `Expr` `82-423` (340); states `426-606` (180); `Transition` and the helpers `608-916` (310); `outcome`/`FlowSum` `918-1008` (90); dwell times `1010-1071` (60); `Graph` `1099-1923` (825, of which `describe/edges/influences/layout/diagram/equations/flow_terms/to_json` `1501-1816` is 315 of presentation); formatting and `TransitionTable` `1925-2035` (110); `by`/`TimeValue`/`timeseries`/`seasonal` `2043-2428` (385); `geo`/`gravity`/`Mobility` `2430-2710` (280, including a CSV register loader with its own validation, `2619-2680`); `Strat`/`Transfer`/`stratify` `2712-2972` (260). Move `by`, `timeseries`, `seasonal` (and `par`) to `parameters.py` (spec 04); `Strat`, `stratify`, `Transfer` to `dimensions.py` (05); `geo`, `gravity`, `Mobility` to `networks.py` or a `space.py` (06 owns mobility); the `Graph` presentation methods plus `format_expr`, `quantity_str`, `quantity_json`, `TransitionTable` to `inspect.py`. What remains is `Expr`, `State`, `Transition`, the helpers, `outcome`, dwell times, and `Graph.collect/resolve_*/check`: about 1,550 lines, which is the declarative core and nothing else. Single-caller helpers that could be inlined or moved with their caller: `erlang_stages` (1), `func_source` (1), `class_members` (1), `influences`/`layout` (diagram only), `flow_terms` (equations and export), `uninfected` (strain only).

10. **Spec-versus-implementation gaps in this area that the departures list does not mention.** (a) `ss.func` does not exist (`'func' in dir(ss)` is `False`), though 03 §Custom per-step behavior describes it and 17 counts it. (b) `ss.immigration` and `ss.emigration` are in 03 §Births but not in 17's fourteen names, so the vocabulary count is wrong by two. (c) `ss.Expr`, `ss.Graph`, `ss.Strat`, `ss.Transfer`, `ss.Cond`, `ss.CodedError`, `ss.error`, `ss.note` are exported (`__init__.py:130-136`) although 17 §Removed says `ss.Expr` went and none of the others is counted; of these only `ss.Strat` appears in a test, once. (d) `sir.infection.beta` (finding 5). (e) `ss.transfer(p=)` (finding 4). (f) `ss.Transition(beta=, source=)` keywords (finding 3). (g) 03 §The operators promises that `S >> p * A + (1 - p) * B @ rate` "says to put parentheses around the destinations"; the actual message is "A transition (Transition(-> B @ 0.1)) cannot be used inside a rate; did you mean one of its states?", which points the wrong way. (h) `ss.transmission` takes `where=` (`806`), not in 17's keyword table. Each is a one-line fix in one direction or the other; the point is that the three documents should agree.

## Other findings

- **Four demographic helpers for two booleans.** `birth`/`immigration` differ by `per_capita` and `migration`; `death`/`emigration` differ by `migration` only (`844-915`). The agent engine reads `tr.migration` in two places. If the names stay (they do read well), the flags should not be `Transition` attributes but, say, `kind='birth' | 'immigration'` on the inflow; if the vocabulary should shrink, `ss.birth(rate, newborn=False)` and `ss.death(rate, leaving=True)` are two names instead of four. Either way 17 needs the names it counts.
- **`Selector.per_person` is a second parameter-ladder evaluator.** `conditions.py:425-446` handles `by`, callables, `TimePar`, `Arr`, and scalars for coverage and amounts; `Engine.value` (`solvers.py:834-860`) handles the same ladder for rates, with `Dist` means, dicts, and modifiers on top. They will drift (the first does not apply modifiers or `Dist`). One `ladder(value, ctx)` in `parameters.py` would serve both.
- **`dwell_stats` dispatches on the distribution's class name as a string** (`transitions.py:1021-1043`: `name == 'expon'`, `'gamma'`, ...) with a sampling fallback. If `ss.Dist` subclasses carried `mean`/`std` (SciPy's frozen distributions already do), this is five lines and cannot go stale when a distribution is renamed.
- **Branching has two representations the engines must both handle.** A multi-destination transition with `p=[...]` (`'I -> R | D'`, or `S >> (p*A + (1-p)*B)`) and several single-destination transitions each with `p=` (`resolve_exits` `1448-1481` sorts the second kind into `branches`, the first stays a transition with `len(dst) > 1`). The spec sanctions both spellings, but the engines could see one: `Graph` could split a multi-destination transition into per-branch transitions that share a name (results summed, `.select(to=)` still selectable). I have not verified how much engine code this would remove; `tracks`/`immediate` in `flow_terms` (`1716-1726`) suggests a fair amount.
- **`E205` for "one `p=` branch plus two timed exits" is misleading.** `ss.Disease(a=progression('I -> H', p=0.2, dur=3), b=progression('I -> R', dur=5), c=progression('I -> D', dur=9))` says "the branch probabilities out of state I sum to 0.2, not 1", when the problem is that the remainder rule (`1475-1480`) needs exactly one timed transition to give the rest to. The message should say which transitions could take the remainder.
- **`Graph.add`'s reserved-name list is hardcoded twice** (`reserved` at `1118` and the literal list at `1208`); `dir(ss.Module)` already covers most of it.
- **`note()` deduplicates by exact string** (`66`), so a note that includes a number (`'... approximated by 11 Erlang stages'`) repeats for every transition and run; keying on `(code, where)` would be more robust.
- **`Strat.__init__` is an 80-line validator** (`2727-2804`) doing kind inference, geo unpacking, mobility coercion, bin labelling, carriage, adjust, immunity, and split; each is sound, but a reader needs the whole method to know what a `Strat` is. It also special-cases `age` given as labels into `age_group` (`2863`) and `kind='plain'` (`2764-2766`), which the spec does not mention.
- **`Transition.__init__` quietly turns `move=` arrows into a `where=`** (`671-675`), so `move=dict(strain='A+B -> A')` with a user `where=` ANDs the two. That is right, but it means `tr.where` is no longer what the user wrote, and `describe()` does not print it.
- **`Disease.__init__` decides "declared or Starsim 3" by scanning kwargs for declared types** (`diseases.py:73`). A Starsim 3 subclass passed an `ss.par` keyword silently flips into graph mode. A class attribute (`declarative = True`, set by `class_members` finding anything, or by the presence of states) would be explicit.

## Things that are good

- `evaluate()` with pluggable contexts, and JAX reusing it through `ctx.xp` (`gradients.py:762`): one expression language, not three. Do not add a JAX-specific tree.
- `linear()` (`343-392`) and the `E208`/`E211` rules: "a bare state means exposure" is enforced at build, with the right message, and it is what makes networks and the ODE the same model text.
- The operator precedence design: `I @ beta` returning a sourceless transition that forwards arithmetic to its rate (`730-737`, `486-488`) made every expression in the spec parse as intended without parentheses, including `S >> I @ beta * I + beta_vax * I_vax`, `(S | I | R) >> D @ mu`, `I >> ss.null @ 0.02`, `ss.null >> S @ r * N.n`, and `I >> 0.1 * H @ ss.days(5)`.
- Lazy conditions are genuinely one mechanism on agents and on groups: `Cond.eval` with `Selector` providing leaves of shape `(n_agents,)` or `(states, groups)` (`conditions.py:214-423`), with `check_edge` turning "70 is inside the 65+ bin" into a precise `E804`.
- `Graph.resolve_exits` (`1448-1481`) states the competing-hazards rule (branches split on entry, rates compete, one remainder) in twenty lines; `describe()` marks every guess; `TransitionTable.__repr__` prints the matrix the spec asks for.
- Stable error codes (`E204`, `E210`, ...) with the fix in the message. Keep the `codes` table.
- `__set_name__` on `State`, `Transition`, `par`, `outcome` for the class form: zero magic, and the per-instance deep copy (`diseases.py:88`) means two instances do not share states (checked).

## Questions for Cliff

- Merge `Cond` into `Expr` (finding 1), or keep two trees and only add the guard at the seam?
- Should arithmetic on a finished transition ever change its rate (finding 2), or is `k * infection` always a flow weight?
- Should `Transfer` become `Transition(move=)` (finding 4), and should `ss.transfer(coverage=...)` keep returning a `Delivery`, or should the intervention form be spelled on the delivery side (`ss.vaccinate(move=...)`-style)?
- Is `|` on states worth keeping as a separate concept, or is "a set of states" the same as "a sum of states" (finding 6)?
- Keep the `Transition.dur` setter magic and add one for `rate`, or drop it and route all value changes through `pars` (finding 5)?
- Split `transitions.py` along the spec's chapters (finding 9)? The public names do not change; only `import` paths inside the package do.
- Should `immigration`/`emigration` stay as names (then 17 needs them), or fold into `birth`/`death` keywords?
