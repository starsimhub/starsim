# Interventions, effects, demographics, and mixing: review

I read `design/06-routes.md`, `07-effects.md`, `08-interventions.md`, the best-practices `interventions.md`, `composition-and-effects.md`, `population-and-mixing.md`, `admin/implementation.md` and `deferred.md`, and the code in `starsim/interventions.py`, `products.py`, `connectors.py`, `conditions.py`, `demographics.py`, `networks.py`, `named.py`, the mixing and modifier parts of `solvers.py`, and `geo`/`gravity`/`Mobility`/`Strat`/`transfer` in `transitions.py`, with `tests/test_deliveries.py` and `tests/test_networks.py` as the spec of what works; every claim below that says "verified" was checked by running the v4 Python. The verdict: the v4 core here is well designed and small (ten names a user needs: `Vaccine`, `Test`, `Treatment`, `vaccinate`, `screen`, `treat`, `modify`, `change`, `transfer`, `Intervention`), the `Delivery`/`Selector` mechanism that makes one declaration run on agents and on groups is the right architecture and should not be cut, and the geography and named-data pieces are minimal and good. The costs are at the edges: the same namespace exports 33 intervention and product names and 25 network names, of which the v3 half is agent-only, undocumented for v4, and in two cases misleading; three delivery keywords are two spellings of one thing (`on=` is exactly `eligible=` plus a round every step, and `years=` means days in a day sim); products carry a second vocabulary for the same targets `ss.modify` uses; and `ss.contacts()` is built as 256 per-agent arrays that the compartmental engine then reverses.

## Top findings

1. **`on=` is exactly `eligible=` plus `interval=` of one step; `years=`/`dates=` are one keyword, misnamed.** Verified: `ss.screen(test, on=sis.I, coverage=0.1)` and `ss.screen(test, eligible=sis.I, interval=1, coverage=0.1)` give identical `delivered` series under `ode` and `abm`, and so do `on=sis.infection` and `eligible=sis.infection, interval=1` (a transition is accepted by `eligible=` too). The code shows why: `step()` passes `selector.select(self.on)` where it would pass `everyone()` (`starsim/interventions.py:900`), and `offer()` then multiplies by `eligible` (`:977`); the only other effect of `on=` is to turn off the routine "offered once" memory (`:627`). `years=[5]` in a sim with `unit='day'` delivers on day 5 (verified; `connectors.py:141-152` reads a bare number in the sim's unit), `dates=` is merged into the same list (`interventions.py:584`), and `ss.change` already spells the same idea `at=`. Proposal: one schedule vocabulary across `ss.change`, deliveries, and `ss.modify`: `at=` for rounds (replacing `years=`/`dates=`), `interval=`, `start=`/`stop=`; keep `on=` only for what `eligible=` cannot say well, which is nothing, so either drop it or restrict it to transitions (events) and reject states and conditions, which is where `on=sis.I` misleads ("every step, everyone in I" is not an event). What breaks: the keyword names in `08-interventions.md`, `tests/test_deliveries.py`, and the `on=` code path (`:598, :627, :700, :738, :814, :900, :940, :1205`), which goes.

   ```python
   # before
   ss.vaccinate(bcg, coverage=0.7, years=[2021, 2025], eligible=ppl.age < 5)
   ss.screen(hiv_test, on=anc.visit, coverage=0.9, on_positive=ss.treat(art))
   # after
   ss.vaccinate(bcg, coverage=0.7, at=[2021, 2025], eligible=ppl.age < 5)
   ss.screen(hiv_test, eligible=anc.visit, interval=ss.days(1), coverage=0.9, on_positive=ss.treat(art))   # or keep on= for transitions only
   ```

2. **The common case should not need a product object: `ss.vaccinate(efficacy=0.85, coverage=0.7, at=[2021, 2025])`.** The product/delivery split is right for reuse (one `bcg` in three deliveries) and for tests (a result per person), but every example in the spec and tests writes `ss.vaccinate(ss.Vaccine(efficacy=0.9), ...)`, two constructors for one thing, and the product is then named by frame inspection two levels up (`interventions.py:574-581`). Each delivery class already knows its product kind (`vaccinate.kind = ss.Vaccine`, `:1234`), so `Delivery.__init__` can build `self.kind(**product_kwargs)` when `product=None` and `efficacy=`/`sensitivity=`/`waning=`/`cost=` are given, named after the delivery. About 15 lines; nothing breaks, since the explicit form stays. While there: `ss.Vaccine()` with no `efficacy` builds fine and crashes at run time with `TypeError: float() argument ... 'NoneType'` (verified; `products.py:264` computes `1 - None`); require `efficacy=` at construction.

   ```python
   # before
   bcg = ss.Vaccine(efficacy=0.85, waning=ss.years(5))
   ss.vaccinate(bcg, coverage=0.7, years=[2021, 2025])
   # after (the explicit form still works)
   ss.vaccinate(efficacy=0.85, waning=ss.years(5), coverage=0.7, at=[2021, 2025])
   ```

3. **Products speak a different target vocabulary from `ss.modify` and `adjust=` for the same quantities.** A vaccine's efficacy keys are `acquisition`, `transmission`, `mortality`, or a transition name (`products.py:370-381`); `ss.modify` and `stratify(adjust=)` take `rel_sus`, `rel_trans`, or a transition name. Verified: `ss.Vaccine(efficacy=dict(rel_sus=0.5))` raises `E505 ... Valid keys are acquisition, transmission, ...` and `stratify(risk=..., adjust=dict(acquisition=...))` raises `E301 ... (and it can also adjust rel_trans and rel_sus)`. The spec says a product "is realized as modifiers" (08 §Products), so it should name what a modifier names. Proposal: products take `rel_sus`, `rel_trans`, and transition names, exactly as `ss.modify` does; keep `acquisition`/`transmission` as accepted synonyms if the prose wants them, and keep `mortality` as the one bundle that has no modifier spelling (every death transition). What breaks: the three key names in `08-interventions.md`, `test_vaccinate`, `test_treat`, and tbsim's products.

4. **`ss.Treatment` means two different things depending on the Python type of `efficacy`.** A number is "cures with this probability by moving the person along a recovery transition" and a dict is "a held product that scales rates", and the class is `Treatment(Held)` whose `holds` property flips on `isinstance(self.efficacy, dict)` (`products.py:454-456`). With the default `efficacy=1.0` and `transition='protect'`, a treatment becomes a general "move these people along any transition" (`tests/test_deliveries.py:563-572`), which is a transfer along the disease graph wearing a clinical name. A user who writes `efficacy=dict(recovery=0.5)` expecting "cures half" gets "halves the recovery rate for as long as the product is held", with no error. Proposal: separate the two by keyword, not by type: `ss.Treatment(cure=0.95, transition=None)` for the one-shot kind and `ss.Treatment(efficacy=dict(...), waning=...)` for the held kind (both allowed together for a drug that cures some and suppresses the rest). Then `Held` is only ever held, and `Delivery.give()`'s three-way branch on product type (`interventions.py:1093-1108`) becomes two.

5. **`Effect` is a second implementation of `ss.modify`.** `products.Effect` (`products.py:229-268`) carries `op`, `active`, `apply`, `name`, `tabulated`, `constant`, and `describe` so that `connectors.combine()` (`connectors.py:164-178`) can duck-type it next to real modifiers, and its `apply` is `modify.apply` with `where=has_<product>` and `multiply=1-efficacy` written out by hand (`:253-264` against `connectors.py:293-302`). The spec's own words are "a product is realized as modifiers". Proposal: `Held.init_pre` appends `ss.modify(target, multiply=1 - eff, where=<the product's dimension>, name=product.name)` objects (with the waning factor folded into the amount as a function where waning is a function), and `Effect` goes; `what_modifies()`, `tabulated`, and `describe` then come for free, and the `has_<product>` dimension is a static condition so the whole-run kernels keep working. What is lost: nothing user-facing.

6. **`ss.contacts()` builds a contact matrix as 256 agent-level modules and the compartmental engine then reverses the construction.** `ss.contacts('Kenya')` returns an `ss.MixingPools` of 16×16 `MixingPool` modules, each with its own per-agent `eff_contacts` FloatArr (`networks.py:2053-2055`), its own `bernoulli`, and a `step()` that recomputes its source and destination UIDs; verified: 256 pools and 205 MB of `eff_contacts` at 100,000 agents, for one 16×16 matrix. The compartmental engine then recovers the matrix with `np.vectorize(self.mean)` over the `ss.constant` cells (`solvers.py:211-213`), and the model spec cannot rebuild the matrix from the pools (`deferred.md`, "The network of an `ss.contacts()` given as a matrix is written in the model spec as the mixing pools it makes"). The agent engine already has a path that uses the matrix directly from the agents' counts per group, but only when the model has places (`Mixing.init_pools()`, `solvers.py:286-310`; used at `:4015-4025`). Proposal: `ss.contacts()` returns a small `Contacts` route holding `matrix`, `age`/`dims`, and `ref`; the agent engine applies it through the counts-per-group path in every model, not only with places; `MixingPools` stays as the v3 class for pools defined by `src`/`dst` functions. Then the three names answer the brief's question: `Mixing` (`solvers.py:46`) is internal and should stay so; `ss.MixingPool(n_contacts=c)` is the one scalar case; `ss.contacts()` is the matrix case; they are not one concept with three names, but today two of them are one concept with two representations.

7. **The v3 intervention and product classes are 24 exports and about 700 lines that v4 does not need, are agent-only, and in two cases mislead.** `RoutineDelivery`, `CampaignDelivery`, `BaseTest`, `BaseScreening`, `routine_screening`, `campaign_screening`, `BaseTriage`, `routine_triage`, `campaign_triage`, `BaseTreatment`, `treat_num`, `BaseVaccination`, `routine_vx`, `campaign_vx` (`interventions.py:77-528`) and `Dx`, `Tx`, `Vx`, `simple_vx` (`products.py:31-225`) are the "class per combination" the spec rejects (08 §Rejected), only run as agents, and are the only interventions the user guide documents (`docs/user_guide/modules_interventions.qmd:18-46`; no `.qmd` mentions `ss.vaccinate` or `ss.modify`). `ss.Vx(efficacy=0.9)` returns an `ss.Vaccine` that is not an `ss.Vx` (verified: `isinstance(ss.Vx(efficacy=0.9), ss.Vx)` is `False`), because `__new__` returns another class (`products.py:37-40, 109-112, 173-176`). `ss.seasonality` (`connectors.py:24-106`) does `disease.rel_trans[:] = rel_beta` every step, the clobbering anti-pattern the spec is built to remove, and verified it forces a declared model to `abm` ("seasonality only works on agents"), while `ss.seasonal` does the same job as a parameter form; two names one letter apart. Proposal: move the v3 delivery classes and `simple_vx` to `starsim.library` (or delete them), make `Vx = Vaccine`, `Dx = Test`, `Tx = Treatment` plain aliases, and replace `ss.seasonality` by a one-line example of `ss.modify(dis.rel_trans, multiply=ss.seasonal(...))` in the docstring. What is lost: `Dx(df=)`'s multi-outcome result hierarchies (HPVsim-style `positive/inadequate/negative`) and `treat_num`'s carried-over queue; the first could be `ss.Test(results=...)` later, and the second is `capacity=` without a queue today (the best-practices open question "stock that carries over").

8. **The register of movements is more general than `Mobility` lets it be.** `ss.Mobility(data=...)` is a table of dated events that add, remove, or move people by place and state, with `to_states` (`transitions.py:2554-2680`), and it is the one declared form of "scheduled events on counts" in v4. `deferred.md` lists three gaps that are this same table with a different column: "seeding a fixed number of people on a schedule", "a one-off move between states at given times (camdl's `transfer`)", and life-table deaths. Proposal: lift it out as `ss.events(table)` (or `ss.schedule`), where `from`/`to` name a state and/or a level of any dimension, accepted on a disease or in `interventions=`; `Mobility(data=)` becomes an alias or goes. On the brief's wider question: `Mobility` is three things in one coat, and two of them already have homes: `commute`/`visit`/`force` are a contact matrix between places (`Mixing.time_share`, `term_exposure` at `solvers.py:271-284`, `within_places` at `:168-183`), and `migrate` is a transfer along the patch dimension at matrix rates (`Mixing.migration`). The user surface `sir.stratify(patch=kenya, mobility=ss.gravity(k=0.5))` is minimal and should stay; the point is that the register does not belong to places. Also: `kind='visit'` exists in the code (`transitions.py:2580, 2596`) and in `implementation.md` but not in `06-routes.md`, which has `commute`, `migrate`, and `force`; either document it or cut it.

9. **Two features that keep a compartmental run faithful cost a dimension each and about 150 lines; ask whether "run as agents" would do.** A test `delay=` in a compartmental run adds a `pending_<delivery>` dimension with `2m+1` levels, `m` the delay in steps (`interventions.py:645-682, 903-921, 1143-1175`): verified, a 3-day delay gives 7 levels and a toy SIS with one screen goes to 14 groups. Protection that wanes by a function of time adds 10 levels of time since delivery (`products.py:279, 325-356`) and a note. Both multiply every other dimension, and a routine delivery with `coverage<1` or `capacity` adds an `offered_<delivery>` dimension as well (`interventions.py:635-638, 680-681`; verified in `describe()`). They work and agree with agents to 1.5%, so this is a cost question, not a bug: keep exponential waning (one level) and `offered_`, and consider making `delay=` and function-shaped waning `E804` under compartmental solvers (`solver='auto'` then picks agents, as it does for `approx=False` today at `sim.py:973`). What is lost: tbsim's care cascade with delayed results as an ODE, if it uses `delay=`.

10. **The spec specifies two things the code does not do, and should be cut rather than built.** (a) `dependence='density'` and `n_ref` on `ss.MixingPool` (`06-routes.md` §Frequency and density dependence; `17-vocabulary.md:175`): verified `ss.MixingPool(n_contacts=10, dependence='density')` raises "unrecognized arguments"; `implementation.md:285` says "there is no density-dependent mixing" only as an aside under the interop departure, and `beta * I.n` already writes it. (b) The sexual-network approximation paragraph (06 §Routes, "The model is stratified by the attributes the network forms partnerships on ... partner-change rates"): the code approximates `PartnerNet` by homogeneous mixing with the mean degree as the contact rate (`solvers.py:149-160`; verified note "PartnerNet approximated by homogeneous mixing with 0.7 contacts per day") and `MFNet` raises `E804` (verified). Both are listed as done in `implementation.md` ("Networks, as agents and (approximated, with a note) as compartments") without saying how. Cut (a) from the spec and vocabulary, and replace (b) with the one sentence the code implements.

## Other findings

- **A follow-up delivery silently ignores its own schedule.** `ss.treat(drug, coverage=0.8, years=[100], start=200)` passed as `on_positive=` delivers whenever the parent does (verified: steps 0, 7, 14, ... with no note), because `step()` returns early for `triggered` deliveries (`interventions.py:894`). Raise at `init_pre` when a follow-up has `start`, `stop`, `years`, `dates`, `interval`, `on`, or `deliver`.
- **`capacity=500` means 500 per round in a campaign and 500 per unit time in routine delivery** (`interventions.py:1060-1061`; verified: 100 per round against 50 per half-day step). The meaning of one keyword depends on another. A bare number should be a count (per round, or per step), and a rate should be typed (`ss.perday(500)`), with an error for a bare number in routine delivery, which is the ambiguous case.
- **`ss.change` is `ss.modify(target, multiply=ss.timeseries({...}, mode='constant'))`** (verified identical `n_I`). It is 60 lines (`connectors.py:339-400`) and more legible than the long form, so keep it, but it is a third spelling of a timed modifier next to `start=`/`stop=`; the spec should say it is sugar and `at=` should be the schedule keyword everywhere (finding 1).
- **The string-path target is a symptom, and the error message hides the cure.** `ss.modify(school.pars.n_contacts, ...)` on a mixing pool raises `E504 ... not ss.constant(v=10) (constant). A plain number has no name` (verified) without mentioning `'school.pars.n_contacts'`, and the path form accepts only `n_contacts` for a network (`connectors.py:243-258`) although 07 says any parameter of any module. The underlying cause is that v3 modules' `pars` hold plain values while declared modules hold `ss.par` objects that know their module and name; until `define_pars` wraps values the same way, at least name the path form in the error, and accept `ss.modify(school, 'n_contacts', ...)` as the object spelling.
- **Two demographic systems.** `ss.Births`, `ss.Deaths`, `ss.Pregnancy` (`demographics.py`, 1,175 lines, unchanged from v3) are agent-only (verified `E804` under `ode`, and `solver='auto'` goes to agents), while v4 models declare births and deaths as transitions *inside a disease* (`ss.Disease(..., births=ss.birth(...))`; `03-states-and-transitions.md:341-360`), which puts a fact about the population on one disease; with two declared diseases each declares its own and the crossing keeps them consistent (verified). Accepting `ss.birth`/`ss.death` in `ss.Sim(demographics=...)` for declared models would give one place for the simple case; `ss.death(rate=ss.by(age=[...]))` then covers most of what `ss.Deaths` with age-specific rates does, and `deferred.md`'s "deaths from a life table have no declared form" is the remaining gap.
- **`ppl = ss.People(1)` as a schema object is a wart** (known issue; verified `ss.People()` needs `n_agents`). Since `sir.age < 15` already works once `age` is a stratification (verified), the hack is only needed for unstratified per-agent conditions; allow `ss.People()` with no size, which is what the tests mean by `ss.People(1)`.
- **Routes carry a second `beta`.** `MixingPool(beta=)`, `contacts(beta=)` ("a multiplier on transmission through these contacts", `networks.py:2161, 2015`), and per-edge `beta` on `RandomNet`/`PartnerNet` multiply the disease's `beta` (`solvers.py:140, 148`), while 06 says infectiousness belongs to the disease, per route via a dict. In the compartmental engine the product is folded into the printed contact rate (`contacts=mean(n_contacts)*beta`), so `describe()` shows 7 contacts a day for `n_contacts=10, beta=0.7` without saying why. For declared models, warn on a route `beta != 1` and point to `beta=dict(<route>=...)` on the transmission.
- **`MFNet`'s targeted mode duplicates `PartnerNet`.** `MFNet(n_contacts=, concurrency=, max_contacts=)` adds a second code path inside `MFNet` (`networks.py:1484-1657`: `targeted`, its own RNGs, `pair_sexes`, a different `init_post` and `add_pairs`) that is `PartnerNet` restricted to opposite-sex pairs. On the brief's question: cross-sectional degree targets with concurrency and a cap are how STI network data is reported, not an EpiModel-only feature, so `PartnerNet` passes principle 9; what fails it is having it twice. Give `PartnerNet` `between='sex'` (or let `match=` tilt below chance, which `match_pairs` at `:1099-1104` currently cannot) and `acts=` (which `deferred.md` says it lacks), and cut the three keywords from `MFNet`, which stays the v3 network. `net.check()` is good and stays.
- **`ss.Test` says what it detects two ways.** `sensitivity=dict(I=..., E=...)` "then detects being in those states" and `detects=cond` are both ways to name the condition (`products.py:486-489`, `interventions.py:1115-1129`); one of them (`detects=`, with `sensitivity` a number or `ss.by`) is enough.
- **The spec's open question on vaccination is still open in the code and docs.** `ss.transfer(vaccinated=True, coverage=0.7, ...)` after `stratify(vaccinated=..., adjust=...)` and `ss.vaccinate(ss.Vaccine(efficacy=0.85), ...)` are both documented ways (08 §Simple cases, best-practices `interventions.md` §Open questions), and `ss.transfer()` returns a `Transfer` or a `Delivery` by keyword (`transitions.py:2961-2967`; verified). They are already one mechanism underneath (a `Delivery` with `move=`, and a product is a dimension plus modifiers), so this is a documentation decision: make `ss.vaccinate` the documented way and present `transfer`-with-coverage once, as the general primitive.
- **`implementation.md` contradicts itself on geographies.** The departures list says "`ss.geo` takes the places directly ...; there are no named geographies" (`implementation.md:277`) while the section above says named geographies for 184 countries are bundled (`:117`) and `named.py` implements them. Delete the stale line.
- **Docs are v3-only for this area.** Not my area, but it bears on every finding above: no tutorial or user-guide page uses `ss.vaccinate`, `ss.screen`, `ss.treat`, `ss.modify`, `ss.change`, `ss.contacts`, `ss.geo`, or `ss.Mobility`; the only intervention page documents the classes finding 7 proposes to remove.

## Things that are good

- **`Delivery` as one class, with `Selector` (`conditions.py:214`) deciding whether a selection is a boolean per agent or a fraction per state and group.** This is what makes `ss.vaccinate(...)` one declaration under `ode`, `binomial`, and `abm`, with results by stratum in each; it should not be split into agent and compartmental versions. The ~20 `if selector.abm` branches inside it are the price, and they are cheaper than two classes.
- **Lazy conditions** written against real attributes (`(ppl.age > 65) & ~sir.vaccinated`), printed as written, with the try-it-and-report rule for groups (`E804` naming the bin edge). No global predicate vocabulary was the right call.
- **Base and effective values with `what_modifies()`**, and `track_writes=True` off by default (`arrays.py:915-958`, `solvers.py:868-975`): the combination rule is stated, products and stratification adjustments go through it, and clipping is reported once.
- **`ss.change(at=, by=)`**: the Covasim one-liner, back.
- **`ss.geo('Kenya', level=1)`, `ss.contacts('Kenya', layer='school')`, `ss.people('Kenya')`** with provenance in the spec and hash (`named.py`): small surface, bundled data, no dependency on another framework's package.
- **`sir.stratify(patch=kenya, mobility=ss.gravity(k=0.5))`** as the whole spatial surface, with `kind` guessed and printed.
- **The whole-run tabulation of scheduled deliveries (`Delivery.tabulated`, `shifts()`)** so that a sim with a campaign still runs in one kernel call and can be differentiated.
- **`net.check()`** and the degree results on `PartnerNet`: EpiModel's mandatory diagnostic as one call.
- **Follow-ups as deliveries of their own** (`on_positive=ss.treat(...)`), each with its results, named `<parent>_positive`.

## Questions for Cliff

- Finding 1: should `on=` go, or stay restricted to transitions (events)? It reads well in `ss.screen(test, on=anc.visit, ...)`, but it is implemented as `eligible=` plus a round every step, and on a state it misleads.
- Finding 7: delete the v3 intervention and product classes, or move them to `starsim.library`? STIsim and HPVsim depend on `routine_vx`, `routine_screening`, `Dx(df=)`, and `treat_num`; a library home keeps them importable without being the first thing a v4 user sees.
- Finding 9: are compartmental test delays and function-shaped waning worth their dimensions, or should they be agents-only with `solver='auto'` choosing agents? Which tbsim or vignette models rely on them as ODEs?
- Finding 8: is a general `ss.events(table)` (scheduled adds, removes, and moves between states and levels) wanted, given that it would close three `deferred.md` gaps at once, or should the register stay spatial?
- Demographics: should `ss.Sim(demographics=[ss.birth(...), ss.death(...)])` be accepted for declared models, so that births are declared once for the population rather than once per disease?
- Route-level `beta`: deprecate it for declared models (warn and point to `beta=dict(route=...)` on the transmission), or keep it as the v3 convention?
- `MFNet` targets: cut the three keywords and extend `PartnerNet` with `between='sex'` and `acts=`, or keep both?
