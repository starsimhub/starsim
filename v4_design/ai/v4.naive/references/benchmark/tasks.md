# Modeling tasks

Use Starsim 4 (`import starsim as ss`) for every task. Time is in days unless stated. "Seeds" are people infected (infectious) at the start. Unless stated, use a deterministic run if Starsim offers one. Report each answer as a single number.

- T1. An SIR epidemic in a population of 100,000, with R0 = 2.5, a mean infectious period of 7 days, and 10 seeds. What fraction of the population has ever been infected (including the seeds) after 365 days?
- T2. An SEIR epidemic in a population of 1,000,000: R0 = 3, a mean latent period of 5 days, a mean infectious period of 7 days, and 100 seeds (infectious, not latent). On what day is the number of infectious people highest?
- T3. An SIRS model: R0 = 3, a mean infectious period of 5 days, and immunity that lasts 1 year on average (exponentially distributed). What fraction of the population is infectious at the endemic equilibrium (run long enough, e.g. 20 years)?
- T4. A measles-like SIR model with births and deaths balanced so the population is constant, a life expectancy of 50 years, a transmission rate of 10/14 per day, and a mean infectious period of 14 days. What fraction of the population is susceptible at equilibrium (e.g. averaged over years 180 to 200)?
- T5. Two age groups: children (30% of a population of 1,000,000) and adults (70%). A child has 10 contacts per day with children and 3 with adults; an adult has 2 contacts per day with children and 6 with adults. The chance of infection per contact with an infectious person is 0.02, the mean infectious period is 7 days, and at the start 0.01% of each group is infectious. What fraction of children, and what fraction of adults, are infected over 365 days? (Two numbers.)
- T6. An SIR epidemic with R0 = 2, a 7-day infectious period, a population of 1,000,000, and 100 seeds. On day 0, 30% of the population (chosen at random, excluding nobody) receives a vaccine that fully protects 90% of recipients and does nothing for the rest. How many infections does the vaccine avert over 365 days, compared with no vaccine?
- T7. An SIR epidemic with R0 = 2.5, a 7-day infectious period, a population of 1,000,000, and 100 seeds. Schools close from day 30 to day 90, which cuts transmission by 40% for that period. How many people are infected over 365 days (not counting the seeds)?
- T8. The file `t8_weekly_cases.csv` (in this folder) has weekly reported cases of an outbreak in a closed population of 100,000, starting from 20 infectious people on 2025-01-06. About 30% of infections are reported, and the mean infectious period is 6 days. Estimate R0.
- T9. In a population of 1,000 with R0 = 2 and a 7-day infectious period, one infectious person is introduced. Using a stochastic model, what is the probability that this leads to a major outbreak (say, more than 10% of the population ever infected)?
- T10. An SIR model where the transmission rate is 1.5 per week and the mean infectious period is 2 weeks, in a population of 10,000 with 10 seeds. Run it for one year with a daily time step. What fraction of the population is ever infected?
